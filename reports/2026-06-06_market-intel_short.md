## The Week in One Line
Nifty slipped **-1.0%** to 23,368 as the RBI held rates and cut its growth forecast, leaving the market cautious and below all major moving averages.

## What's Working
- **Media (+5.3%)**: Big jump driven by heavy trading volume in ZEEL, Network18 and Hathway — a sector starved of attention finally caught a bid.
- **Trading (+4.8%)**: Strong relative strength rotation; small/mid-cap trading and distribution names led as money rotated out of banks.
- **Textiles (+4.2%)**: India-Oman free trade agreement (CEPA) went live this week, opening tariff-free export channels — a direct positive for textile exporters.

## What's Not
- **Cement (-4.1%)**: Weak demand outlook plus a soft UltraTech reaction (-5% over 5 days post results). The sector slipped deeper into the "Lagging" quadrant of the RRG — a chart that maps which sectors are gaining or losing strength vs the index.
- **Financial Services (-2.1%)**: RBI's growth downgrade and an inflation forecast hike hurt the rate-sensitive lenders and NBFCs.
- **FMCG (-1.8%)**: Consumer demand worries continued; ITC's weak earnings still weighing on sentiment.

## Notable Events

**RBI holds rates, downgrades growth.** The Reserve Bank kept the repo rate (the rate at which it lends to banks) unchanged at 5.25%, but cut its FY27 growth forecast to 6.6% and raised the inflation projection to 5.1%. Markets had hoped for a cut to support a weakening rupee and slowing economy — neither arrived. The Sensex fell ~380 points on the decision day.

**US proposes 12.5% tariff on India.** A new US tariff proposal, citing forced-labour concerns, rattled export-heavy sectors like textiles, autos and pharma. This was partly offset later in the week by Trump calling PM Modi a "great friend" and signalling confidence in an eventual trade deal — but the overhang remains.

**FY27 earnings estimates cut by 9%.** Analysts trimmed full-year Nifty profit forecasts sharply this week. Lower expected earnings = lower fair value for the index, which helps explain why Nifty is now **4.8% below its 200-day EMA** (a long-term trend line — being below it usually signals a weak medium-term setup).

## Charts to Know

**Breadth is neutral, not bearish.** Advancing vs declining stocks (how many stocks went up vs down each day) were roughly balanced through the week — 282 up vs 301 down on Friday. But only **44% of stocks** trade above their 200-day EMA. Translation: this isn't a crash, it's a tired market where roughly half the names are in long-term downtrends.

**Sector rotation is happening underneath.** The RRG quadrant flips show 11 sectors moving INTO "Leading" or out of "Weakening" — including Auto Components, Automobiles, Energy, Media and Infrastructure. Meanwhile Cement, Defence and Financial Services moved INTO "Lagging." Money is rotating, not leaving — a healthier sign than broad selling.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings**: The Mar-2026 season is essentially over (only NATCO Pharma reported recently). Focus shifts from results to guidance commentary and management calls.
- **Macro events**: Rupee stability is the big one — the RBI has stepped in to defend it, and any further weakness could force a "surprise rate hike" conversation. Also watch Brent crude (now $95.49, up 4% this week) — sustained moves above $100 hurt India's import bill and inflation.
- **Nifty levels**: Resistance sits at the **20-day EMA around 23,680** (short-term trend) and **50-day EMA at 23,850**. Support to watch is **23,380** — Friday's close. A break below 23,300 could open the door to 23,000.

**One thing to remember as a new investor:** A -1% week with Nifty 5% below its 200-day average isn't panic territory — it's a market digesting weaker earnings expectations and policy uncertainty. Days like these are normal. The rotation underneath (Media, Textiles, Energy gaining; Cement, FMCG losing) is more useful information than the headline index move.
