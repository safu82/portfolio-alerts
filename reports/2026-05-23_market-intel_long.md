## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty 50 closed the week at **23,746.00**, up **+0.4%** from last Sunday's **23,659.35** — a flat-looking print that masks genuine drama underneath. The week was dominated by **US-Iran tensions pushing Brent above $105/bbl mid-week**, which slammed the rupee to record lows and forced the **RBI into heavy dollar-sales intervention** (at least $2bn on Thursday alone, per Reuters). Two domestic policy catalysts then reshaped sectoral flows: India **hiked the gold and silver import duty to 15%** to defend the current account, and the **RBI approved a record ₹2.87 lakh crore dividend** to the government to cushion the fiscal hit from the geopolitical shock. Friday's **+99 point rally** to close the week was driven by easing imported-inflation worries as Brent gave back some gains (-2.2% over the week despite mid-week panic).

## 2. The Trend Picture

The trend remains technically fragile. Per the Nifty 50 Trend Levels:

- **Below the 20-day EMA** (Exponential Moving Average — a smoothed average of recent closes used as a trend reference) of 23,793 by **-0.2%**
- **Below the 50-day EMA** of 23,950 by **-0.9%**
- **Below the 200-day EMA** of 24,618 by **-3.5%**

So Nifty is below all three reference levels — the textbook definition of a stock in a downtrend trying to base. The fact that we're only 0.2% below the 20-EMA after a volatile week is actually a quiet positive: price is coiling tightly under near-term resistance.

Breadth tells a more mixed story. **Only 44% of stocks are above their 200-EMA** — modest improvement from 40% on Monday but still sub-50%. **Monday's session was brutal** (126 advances vs 461 declines on the oil shock); **Tuesday's rebound was textbook** (402 vs 183) as the RBI intervention news landed. No fresh 52-week highs all week, and new 52-week lows held steady at 6-8 per day. Translation: this is a market trying to stabilise, not one breaking out.

## 3. Sector Rotation — Where Money Is Flowing

**Top 3 sectors (5D):** IT **+3.8%**, Aviation **+2.7%**, Realty **+2.2%**.
**Bottom 3:** Logistics **-3.2%**, Media **-3.1%**, FMCG **-1.4%**.

The headline rotation is **IT flipping from Improving to Leading** on the RRG (Relative Rotation Graph — a chart that plots sectors by momentum strength vs the index; "Leading" is the strongest quadrant). After a brutal 1-year (-25.1%) and YTD (-22.2%), IT is showing early stabilisation as the **rupee weakness boosts dollar-earning exporters' rupee revenues**. Wipro and KPIT both feature in the rank-surge list. This is a classic INR-driven defensive rotation.

**Aviation flipped Lagging → Improving** (RS Δ +2.4) as Brent gave back its panic spike — aviation fuel is the largest single cost line for carriers, so a -2.2% weekly Brent print directly compresses cost expectations.

**Auto + Auto Components flipped together** (Auto Leading → Lagging with RS Δ **-5.2**; Auto Components Leading → Weakening). Maruti was flat-to-down post results (-1.8% over 5 days despite a +28% revenue print) — the market is reading peaking cycle, not accelerating one.

**Cement and Chemicals also flipped Leading → Lagging/Weakening** — broad rotation OUT of the prior leadership and INTO defensive INR-plays.

**The gold/silver tell:** the global gold price fell **-1.8%** and silver fell **-5.8%** in USD terms, but Indian gold/silver ETFs would have moved very differently because of the **15% import-duty hike**. That domestic policy lift on landed prices is the kind of divergence that's NOT a "safe-haven bid" — it's a tax-policy effect. METALIETF appearing on the VCP signal list is consistent with this.

**(a) Sector aggregate**: Logistics (+118.9% median EPS YoY) and Telecom (+93.2%) top the list — but Logistics has only 6 reporters so treat with care. **(c) Bellwether large-caps** — the real story:

- **ITC (FMCG)**: EPS **-73% YoY** on -5.0% revenue, stock **-2.1% on day, -2.5% over 5 days**. Genuine fundamental disappointment from a sector heavyweight — reads through to broader FMCG weakness (sector down -1.4% on the week, flipped Weakening → Lagging).
- **Bharti Airtel (Telecom)**: EPS **-38%** despite +15.7% revenue growth, stock **-1.8% over 5 days**. Margin compression at the dominant telco is notable — although the sector aggregate (+93%) looks bullish, that's smaller names like IDEA (+580%); the bellwether is struggling.
- **Sun Pharma (Healthcare)**: +26% EPS, +12.8% revenue, but stock **-2.5% on day, -1.8% over 5 days**. Solid print, weak reaction — suggests the run-up was already priced in. Read-through: be cautious about chasing healthcare on results alone.
- **Titan (Consumer Durables)**: +35% EPS, **+80.5% revenue** (an outstanding number), yet stock **-2.1% over 5 days**. This is the **gold/silver import-duty story directly hitting the tape** — Titan is the most jewellery-exposed bellwether, and the duty hike threatens 2026 demand by 50-60 tonnes per official estimates. The market is looking past Mar 2026 numbers to a tougher H2.
- **Infosys (IT)**: +24% EPS, +13.4% revenue, **+5.0% over 5 days** — the strongest bellwether reaction this week, and the clearest signal behind the IT sector quadrant flip into Leading. TCS (+2.4% 5D) and HCLTECH (+2.8% 5D) confirm the read-through.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The IT rotation is the cleanest story on the tape.** Infosys printing +24% EPS with a +5% weekly reaction, TCS +2.4%, HCLTECH +2.8% — a sector that's been beaten to death (-25% over 1 year) is showing the first genuine sponsorship in months, helped by rupee weakness benefiting dollar earners. Worth watching, but one week doesn't make a trend.

2. **The Titan signal matters more than the headline EPS prints.** A stock with +80% revenue growth and +35% EPS falling -2.1% post-results because of the gold import-duty hike tells you the market is looking *forward* and *policy* is the dominant variable right now — not Mar 2026 numbers. Watch consumer-discretionary breadth into the next month.

3. **Momentum is narrowing, not broadening.** Blue Zone contracted from 64 to 54 stocks, Golden Cross from 10 to 3, while MACD Strong expanded 14 → 22. The signal mix shows the rally is concentrating in fewer, higher-momentum names (TRITURBINE, BALAMINES, SHILPAMED). Honest caveat: that's typical of a recovery in its early stages, but it's also the configuration that precedes failures if the macro shoe drops again. The May 26 RBI swap auction is the next read.
