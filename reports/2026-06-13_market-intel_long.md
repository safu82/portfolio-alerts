## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty 50 closed the week at **23,633.00**, up **+1.1%** from the prior Sunday's 23,368.25 — but the headline number masks a wildly choppy five sessions that ended in Friday's vertical rip (Adv/Dec of **536/49**, the cleanest one-day breadth print in weeks). The dominant cause was a **one-two policy punch**: the US-Iran de-escalation that knocked **Brent down 7.6% to $88.28**, and the RBI's surprise package of **concessional forex swaps plus 7% FCNR(B) NRI deposit rates** targeting $20-30 billion of fresh inflows. Layer in **March retail inflation hitting a 10-month low** (keeping rate-cut hopes alive) and a **new bilateral preferential-tariff deal with the US**, and you have a textbook risk-on Friday. The catch: the rally is narrow at the top, with IT and Metals still bleeding.

## 2. The Trend Picture

Nifty is in a **mixed trend** — above its EMA 20 (Exponential Moving Average — a smoothed average of recent closes used as trend reference) at **23,490** by +0.6%, but BELOW both its EMA 50 (23,733, -0.4%) and EMA 200 (24,454, -3.4%). Translation: short-term momentum is recovering, but the medium- and long-term trends remain broken until Nifty reclaims that 50-day.

Breadth confirms the fragility. Only **45%** of stocks sit above their 200-day EMA as of Friday — better than Monday's 39% but still below 50%. Fresh 52-week lows clocked **29 on Thursday** versus just **1 fresh high all week**, which tells you the rally is being driven by a recovery in beaten-down names rather than new leadership. Friday's A/D split of 536/49 was a stealth-strength day after four sessions of brutal internals (Monday: 70/516, Wednesday: 129/457, Thursday: 128/457). The rotation is happening in real time — and it pivoted on Friday.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The rotation is real but narrow.** Nifty +1.1% on the week masks 4 of 5 sessions with red breadth. The week's gains were concentrated in **Banking, Aviation and FMCG** — the three sectors with clear policy/macro catalysts. Until the EMA 50 (23,733) is reclaimed, treat this as a recovery bounce, not a trend change.

2. **The RBI inflows package is the most important domestic catalyst of the quarter.** It re-rates banks, pressures IT via the rupee, and supports rate-sensitive sectors. Worth watching how foreign deposit flows actually develop versus the $20-30bn target — early Reuters data ($3bn in days) is encouraging but small.

3. **The signal mix is contracting, not expanding.** MACD and Narrow CPR unique counts both halved week-on-week, while Blue Zone Buy expanded only modestly. That's a sign of **rotation between momentum cohorts**, not a broad accumulation phase. Caveat: one strong Friday doesn't refresh the indicators — next week's signal counts will be far more informative.

Honest caveat across all three: the bellwether earnings prints we cited are **weeks old**, so this week's bellwether price moves are pure rotation reads, not fresh earnings reactions. Treat sector implications accordingly.
