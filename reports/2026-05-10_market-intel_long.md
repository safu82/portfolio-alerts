## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty closed the week at **24,173.95**, up **+0.7%** from Sunday's 23,997.55 — a modest headline number that hides a lot of churn underneath. Wednesday's **+291-point surge to 24,340** was the week's signature move, lifted by easing US–Iran tensions and a sharp drop in Brent. But Friday gave back **164 points**, suggesting the market is still digesting the macro picture rather than committing to a clean direction. The dominant theme was **rotation, not breakout** — Healthcare, Aviation and Chemicals leading, while Banking and Metals stalled despite strong earnings.

## 2. The Trend Picture

Nifty is in a **mixed trend** — caught between recovering short-term momentum and a still-broken long-term structure:

- **Above EMA20** (24,032) by +0.6% — short-term: positive
- **Above EMA50** (24,091) by +0.3% — medium-term: just barely positive
- **Below EMA200** (24,581) by -1.7% — long-term: still broken

The EMA20 (Exponential Moving Average — a smoothed average of recent closes that traders watch as a trend reference) and EMA50 are sitting almost on top of each other, which is classic indecision. The index has reclaimed both, but until it clears the 200-day at 24,582, this remains a bounce within a downtrend, not a confirmed reversal.

Breadth is the more encouraging story. **% of stocks above their 200-day EMA climbed from 48% on April 29 to 60% on May 7**, the highest reading in the table. **Wednesday's session was the standout** — 483 advances vs 101 declines (a near 5:1 ratio), the kind of textbook stealth-strength day that often precedes broader participation. Friday's 243/341 split shows the rally isn't unanimous yet. Fresh 52-week highs remained sparse (1 per day at best) — breadth is improving from below, not from new leadership emerging.

## 3. Sector Rotation — Where Money Is Flowing

| Top 3 (5D) | Bottom 3 (5D) |
|---|---|
| Healthcare +5.8% | Banking +0.2% |
| Aviation +5.3% | Telecom +0.7% |
| Trading / Chemicals +4.6% | Energy +1.3% |

The most important quadrant flip on the RRG (Relative Rotation Graph — a tool that maps sectors by momentum and relative strength across four quadrants: Leading, Weakening, Lagging, Improving) is **Automobile flipping from Lagging to Leading** (RS Δ +3.7, momentum Δ +0.40). That's a fresh rotation — and the bellwether earnings (M&M +42% EPS YoY, Bajaj-Auto +103%) tell you why.

Meanwhile, **Defence, Metals, Media and Consumer Durables all dropped from Leading to Weakening** — these were last quarter's winners taking a breather, not collapsing (Defence is still +21% on a 1-month basis). Aviation also flipped Leading → Weakening despite a +5.3% week, which is consistent with **Brent crashing 6% to $101.66** — the fuel-cost relief is being banked rather than chased.

The macro-to-sector links are clean this week: Brent down → Aviation and Auto strong; INR/DXY stable → IT mixed; Gold +2.3% and Silver +5.7% → safe-haven bid persisting alongside equity recovery (a slightly contradictory signal worth noting).

## 4. Earnings Pulse

**Quarter analysed: Mar 2026.** 226 stocks have reported; 317 still pending — so this is roughly a 42% read on the season, decent but not complete.

**(a) Sector aggregate.** The headline read is broadly positive but very uneven. **Realty (+62% median EPS YoY), Conglomerates (+49%), Metals (+43%), Consumer Durables (+40%) and Chemicals (+37%)** are running hot. **Banking is the surprise underperformer at just +9.9% median**, and **Cement is outright negative at -8.5%**. Healthcare's +28.8% median lines up perfectly with its leadership on the price tape — earnings are confirming the rotation.

**(b) Top-of-list extremes.** Wockpharma's +764% EPS, Lloyds Metals +553%, Jindal Steel +408% are eye-catching but mostly low-base effects on smaller caps — useful colour, not sector signals. On the miss side, **Shoppers Stop (-922%) and Sapphire Foods (-750%)** are real warning flags for discretionary consumer services, and explain Shoppers Stop's brutal -46.2 rank-slope decline this week.

**(c) Bellwether large-caps — the real story.**

- **SBIN (-3% EPS, -6.7% on the day, -4.6% over 5 days).** This is the most significant negative reaction of the week. A modest EPS decline shouldn't produce a 6.7% single-day drop unless the market saw something else in the print — likely margin guidance or asset-quality commentary. Read-through: **negative for PSU banks** and a clue to why Banking's median EPS is only +9.9%. Worth flagging that we'd need the conference-call transcript to confirm the driver.

- **TITAN (+35% EPS, +80.5% Rev YoY, +4.7% on the day).** Premium discretionary is alive and well. The revenue print is enormous and the price reaction confirms it — read-through is constructive for Tanishq peers and high-end consumer durables.

- **M&M (+42% EPS, +29% Rev) and BAJAJ-AUTO (+103% EPS, +41% Rev).** Both delivered strong numbers and both were rewarded over 5 days (+7.5% and +7.2%). This is the earnings backbone of the Auto quadrant flip into Leading — the sector rotation isn't speculative, it's earnings-led.

- **RELIANCE (-13% EPS, +12.5% Rev, flat reaction).** A double-digit EPS decline producing essentially no price movement is the market saying "we expected this" — refining margins were the known weak link. Not a sector-mover this quarter.

- **ADANIPORTS (+4% EPS, +26.5% Rev, +6.2% over 5 days).** Modest EPS, strong revenue, and the market liked it — also one of this week's narrow-CPR-breakaway signals (more below). Read-through positive for infrastructure utilisation.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The week was a rotation, not a breakout.** Nifty +0.7% headlines undersells what happened underneath — Auto rotated into Leading, Defence/Metals took a breather, breadth above EMA200 climbed from 48% to 58%. Until Nifty clears EMA200 at 24,582, treat this as a constructive bounce within a broken longer-term trend, not a confirmed turn.

2. **Healthcare is the cleanest setup in the data.** Sector return leadership (+5.8%), earnings median (+28.8%), volume explosions in diagnostics names, and multi-signal triggers across pharma — four independent data sources agreeing. Whether the move continues depends on broadening beyond diagnostics into broader pharma; that's the watch item.

3. **The SBI reaction is the most important single data point this week.** A bellwether PSU bank falling 6.7% on a -3% EPS print suggests the market saw something in the result that the headline number didn't capture — and it's consistent with Banking's weak +9.9% sector median. Caveat: we'd want the call transcript before drawing firm conclusions, but the read-through caution for PSU bank peers is real.
