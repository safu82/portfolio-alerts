## The Week in One Line
Nifty slid **-3.1%** to 22,422 as surging crude, hawkish RBI signals, and broad selling dragged every major sector lower.

## What's Working
Nothing really "worked" this week — every top sector still lost ground, just less than the rest.

- **Aviation (+0.8%)**: The only green sector. Falling jet-fuel worries eased after Brent cooled late in the week, and the sector flipped into the "Leading" quadrant on our relative-strength map (a measure of which sectors are outperforming the broad index).
- **Consumer Services (-1.5%)**: Held up better than peers. Hotel bookings into the festive season kept names like Lemon Tree active (its trading volume was nearly **14x** its normal daily average).
- **Industrial (-2.3%)**: Capex-driven order books cushioned the fall, even as broader risk appetite shrank.

## What's Not
- **Agriculture (-5.8%)**: Hit hardest. A weak monsoon narrative plus the government's **edible-oil import duty cut** (meant to tame festive-season food inflation) squeezed domestic growers' pricing power.
- **Automobile (-5.0%)**: Maruti and M&M both fell ~5% as high crude threatens fuel costs and demand. Bajaj Auto saw a **7x volume spike** — usually a sign of heavy institutional selling.
- **Metals (-4.9%)**: A stronger US Dollar (the DXY rose) typically hurts commodity prices globally, and metals followed suit.

Honourable mention: **FMCG (-4.6%)** and **Cement (-4.7%)** — both heavyweights ITC and HUL fell 4-5% as consumer-demand worries deepened.

## Notable Events

**RBI turns more hawkish.** Bank of America now expects the RBI to raise its policy rate by a full **1 percentage point** through mid-2027. Rate hikes make borrowing costlier, which pressures banks, housing, and auto loans. Add in **$500 million of FX losses** that Indian banks took after new RBI rules on forward currency contracts, and you have a rough week for financials — the sector that usually leads any rally.

**Crude oil shock.** Brent spiked above $106/barrel earlier in the week on US-Iran tensions before settling at **$101**. India imports ~85% of its oil, so expensive crude widens the trade deficit, weakens the rupee, and squeezes margins at paint, tyre, airline, and FMCG companies. The government responded by **cutting excise duty on petrol and diesel by ₹10/litre** — a direct fiscal cushion for consumers, but one that costs the exchequer revenue.

**US tariff reprieve for Pharma.** The US exempted India from its new 100% tariff on select specialty drugs. A clear positive for pharma exporters — look for this to show up in next week's healthcare performance.

## Charts to Know

**The breadth picture is ugly.** "Breadth" simply means how many stocks are participating. On Monday, **510 stocks fell versus only 72 that rose** — that's not a correction in a few names, that's broad selling. By Thursday, only **33% of stocks** were trading above their 200-day average price trend (down from 46% a week earlier). Translation: most stocks have broken down below their long-term uptrend.

**New 52-week lows are climbing.** The number of stocks hitting fresh one-year lows went from **8 on Sep 23 to 79 on Oct 1**. A rising count of new lows is a classic warning sign that selling pressure is spreading, not fading.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings:** The Jun-quarter season is winding down — Sep-quarter results begin trickling in later this month. **TCS, Infosys, and HCL Tech** typically kick off the IT season; their commentary on US client demand will be key.
- **Macro:** Watch **crude oil** (any move back above $105 hurts), the **rupee vs dollar**, and the **UPI MDR rollout on October 15** — this introduces merchant fees on UPI payments above ₹2,000 and could affect fintech names.
- **Nifty levels:** The index is now **3.4% below its 20-day moving average** (a short-term trend line) and **7.1% below its 200-day average** (the long-term trend). First support zone is around **22,200**; a bounce needs to reclaim **23,200** to repair the damage. Until then, treat rallies with caution.
