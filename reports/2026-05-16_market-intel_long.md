## 1. What Happened This Week

The Nifty 50 closed the week at **23,659.35**, down **2.1%** from Sunday's 24,173.95 — but the headline number understates the damage and the recovery. Monday and Tuesday were a near-vertical drop (Nifty fell from 23,812 to 23,427 in two sessions), driven by a **5.2% spike in Brent crude to $106.98** on West Asia tension. Wednesday-Thursday brought a textbook bounce (Thursday alone added ~280 points), but Friday faded again. Underneath, the bigger story was a **wholesale rotation out of leadership**: 13 sectors flipped from "Leading" to "Weakening" on the RRG (Relative Rotation Graph — a chart that maps sectors on momentum vs strength axes, showing which are gaining or losing favour) in a single week. That's not a normal pullback. That's a regime change in progress.

## 2. The Trend Picture

The trend has rolled over, cleanly and across all three timeframes.

| EMA | Level | Nifty position |
|---|---|---|
| 20-day EMA | 23,889.24 | **Below** (-1.0%) |
| 50-day EMA | 24,017.25 | **Below** (-1.5%) |
| 200-day EMA | 24,581.67 | **Below** (-3.8%) |

Nifty is now below its 20, 50 and 200-day EMAs (Exponential Moving Average — a smoothed average of recent closing prices that traders use as a trend reference). The 200-EMA break is the most consequential — that's the line that separates "bull market pullback" from "something more serious."

Breadth confirms the deterioration. The share of NSE 500 stocks trading above their 200-EMA collapsed from **57% on May 7 to just 43% by Friday** — a 14-point drop in six sessions. Tuesday was the capitulation: only **36 advances vs 548 declines** and **13 fresh 52-week lows** with zero new highs. Wednesday-Thursday produced respectable A/D splits (338-244, then 348-235), but Friday slipped back to 202-381. New 52-week lows have outnumbered new highs every single day this week. This isn't broadening — it's narrowing under the surface, even when the index recovers.

## 3. Sector Rotation — Where Money Is Flowing

**Top performers (5-day median):** ETF +4.2%, Telecom +4.1%, Healthcare -0.2%. The first two are defensive — gold/silver ETF flows and Telecom (Bharti +3.9% over five days) absorbing risk-off money. Healthcare's -0.2% is actually a *win* in a -2.1% Nifty week.

**Bottom performers:** Realty -8.9%, Trading -7.6%, Defence -6.5%, Media -6.3%, IT -6.1%. Realty is rate-sensitive and reacts violently to crude/risk-off; Defence got hit by HAL's earnings (more below).

**The rotation tells:**
- **ETF flipped Improving → Leading** (RS Δ +11.3, where RS = Relative Strength rank, lower = stronger). Gold and silver ETFs ripping when crude spikes is textbook risk-off.
- **Banking flipped Leading → Lagging** (RS Δ -6.5) — the most aggressive move of the week. Kotak Bank's rank slope of -37 (improving rank) is a lone bright spot in an otherwise damaged sector.
- **IT flipped Lagging → Improving** — but on momentum (Δ +0.12), not strength. IT is still down 6.1% on the week and -26.7% YTD. This is "less bad," not "good."
- Energy, Infrastructure, Realty, FMCG, Financial Services, Defence, Telecom, Industrial, Logistics, Metals, Textiles, Consumer Services — **all flipped from Leading to Weakening**. When this many sectors flip at once, it's the index doing the work, not genuine rotation.

## 4. Earnings Pulse

The earnings section is filtered to **Mar 2026 (Q4 FY26)**. **330 stocks have reported; 212 are still pending** — so the season is roughly 60% complete and the aggregate picture is meaningful but not final.

**(a) Sector aggregate.** The standouts on median EPS YoY are **Logistics (+119%)**, **Metals (+46%)**, **Consumer Durables (+28%)**, **Financial Services (+26%)** and **Chemicals (+26%)** — all double-digit growers. **Banking (+9.9%)** and **Defence (+5.5%)** are mid-pack-and-fading; **Telecom (+0.9%)** is the weakest median. The metals print is real — the entire sector's median is +46% with 16 stocks reporting, supported by JSW Steel, Jindal Steel and Lloyds Metals all printing triple-digit beats.

**(b) Extremes.** The biggest beats — JSW Steel (+988%), Wockhardt (+764%), Neuland Labs (+665%), Sheela Foam (+597%) — are mostly low-base effects (last year was depressed). Worth noting as colour, not signal. On the miss side, **two consumer services names — Shoppers Stop (-922% EPS YoY) and Sapphire Foods (-750%)** — flag genuine stress in discretionary retail and quick-service restaurants. Crompton (-415%) and VIP Industries (-370%) say the same thing on the durables side.

**(c) Bellwethers — where the real signal lives.**

- **Reliance: EPS -13%, Rev +12.5%, 5-day -6.9%.** A double-digit EPS contraction on healthy topline says **margin compression** — likely refining/petchem hit by the crude spike (Brent +5.2% this week alone is the obvious culprit). The 5-day drawdown was the single biggest weight on the Nifty.
- **Titan: EPS +35%, Rev +80.5%, but 5-day -7.5%.** This is the most interesting print of the week. Revenue **up 80%** and the stock fell 7.5%? Two likely reads: gold prices (-2.4% on the week) are hurting jewellery inventory margins, *and* the print itself was already priced in. The disconnect between the number and the reaction is the story — we'd need the call transcript to confirm, but the read-through for premium discretionary is cautious.
- **HAL: EPS +6%, Rev +1.8%, 1-day -4.8%, 5-day -8.4%.** A near-flat result in a sector that's been priced for 20%+ growth. This single print is most of why **Defence flipped from Leading to Weakening** with the worst sector flip momentum (Δ -0.76).
- **SBI: EPS -3%, 5-day -5.5%.** A PSU bank flagship printing negative EPS YoY is the single best explanation for **Banking flipping Leading → Lagging**. ICICI (+9%) and HDFC Bank (+7%) held up better, but SBI's miss dragged the PSU banking complex.
- **Bharti Airtel: EPS -38%, Rev +15.7%, 5-day +3.9%.** Stock *rallied* on a -38% EPS print. Telecom's Leading-to-Weakening flip is a quadrant artefact, not a fundamental signal — the market clearly looked through the headline EPS (likely one-off / depreciation-heavy) and rewarded the revenue traction.

Infosys (+24%) and TCS (+12%) printed well but **both fell ~5% over five days** — confirming the IT sector is in a "good earnings can't fix bad sentiment" phase.

**Read on the signal mix:** **rotating, not expanding.** MACD signals surging (oversold bounce) while Golden Cross shrinks (no new medium-term uptrends) is what you see in a corrective bounce, not a fresh leg up. Healthcare and metals are doing the heavy lifting on the cluster list — note Neuland, Natco, Biocon, Granules, Fortis, SAIL, Lloyds.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The trend is broken, not just bent.** Nifty below all three EMAs, 13 sector quadrant flips in one week, and breadth (43% above 200-EMA, down from 57%) confirming. The Wednesday-Thursday bounce was an oversold reflex inside a damaged structure — proof in the MACD Buy surge (+16) without a corresponding Golden Cross expansion (-4).
2. **Defensive rotation is the only honest leadership story.** ETF (gold/silver) flipped to Leading, Healthcare held up, Bharti rallied on a bad print. Everything else is either falling or "less bad." The Banking Leading-to-Lagging flip, driven by SBI's miss, is the single most consequential rotation.
3. **Metals + select healthcare are where the technicals and earnings agree.** SAIL's 6-signal cluster sitting on top of a +46% sector EPS print is the cleanest data alignment of the week. Caveat: in a -2.1% Nifty week with 43% breadth, even the strongest setups need confirmation through next week's open — single-stock signals don't override a broken index.
