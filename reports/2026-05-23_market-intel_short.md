## The Week in One Line
Nifty eked out a **+0.4%** gain to close at **23,746**, with IT leading a quiet rebound while a rupee shock and oil spike kept the mood cautious.

## What's Working
- **IT (+3.8%)**: Bounced off deeply oversold levels (still -22% over six months). A weaker rupee actually helps IT exporters, who earn in dollars.
- **Aviation (+2.7%)**: Flipped from Lagging to **Improving** — meaning relative strength is starting to turn up from a low base. Crude easing late in the week (Brent -2.2%) helps fuel-heavy airlines.
- **Realty (+2.2%)**: Bounce from oversold territory. The RBI signaling **no rate hike** despite a weak rupee was a relief — high rates hurt real estate the most.

## What's Not
- **Logistics (-3.2%)**: Sharpest quadrant flip of the week (Weakening → Lagging). Profit-taking after a strong earnings season (sector EPS up **+119%** YoY).
- **Media (-3.1%)**: No earnings tailwind, no positive trigger. The sector keeps drifting lower (-28% over the year).
- **FMCG (-1.4%)**: Hit by a double whammy — **ITC's poor results** (EPS -73%) and news that India may **hike edible oil import duty**, which raises input costs for packaged-food makers.

## Notable Events

**The rupee crisis and RBI's response.** US-Iran tensions pushed crude oil above **$105/bbl**, and the rupee slid to a record low. The RBI stepped in aggressively — selling dollars (over $2 billion in one day), announcing a **$5 billion dollar-rupee swap auction** for May 26, and injecting **₹81,590 crore** of short-term liquidity into banks. Crucially, the RBI signaled it would **not raise interest rates** to defend the rupee, choosing to protect growth instead. Equities took this as a relief.

**India hikes gold and silver import duty to 15%.** This is a domestic policy move to curb the trade deficit — gold imports were widening the current account gap. Notice the unusual setup: **global gold fell -1.8% and silver -5.8% this week**, but Indian gold ETFs likely traded firm because the duty hike pushes up *local* prices. This is a fiscal cause, not a safe-haven move.

**RBI's record ₹2.87 lakh crore dividend to the government.** A massive transfer from the central bank to the Centre, which cushions the government's finances at a time when the oil shock is straining the budget. Bullish for fiscal stability and government-spending-linked sectors.

## Charts to Know

**The breadth tug-of-war.** Advances vs declines — a simple count of how many stocks rose vs fell each day — flipped wildly this week: 126 advances on Monday (terrible), 402 on Tuesday (strong rebound), then near-50/50 for the rest of the week. The percentage of stocks above their 200-day average — a long-term trend gauge — stayed stuck in the **40-45%** range. Translation: the index move was small, but under the surface, less than half of stocks are in long-term uptrends. Participation is narrow.

**IT's rotation back into "Leading."** On a Relative Rotation Graph — which plots a sector's relative strength and momentum vs the broad market — IT moved from the *Improving* quadrant into *Leading*. In plain English: after months of underperformance, IT is now outperforming the market **and** its momentum is picking up. Worth watching, not chasing.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings**: Most of the Mar 2026 season is now reported (415 companies in), but late filers — especially in mid-cap Industrials and Chemicals — can still move stocks. Watch reactions, not just the numbers.
- **Macro**: The **RBI dollar-rupee swap auction on May 26** is the big one — its success will decide whether the rupee stabilizes. Also: any update on edible-oil duty hikes (negative for FMCG), and Brent crude direction tied to US-Iran headlines.
- **Nifty levels**: The index is **below all three key moving averages** (20-day, 50-day, 200-day) — these averages smooth out price to show the trend. The first hurdle is the **20-day EMA at ~23,793** (just above current price). A close above it would be the first sign the short-term downtrend is breaking. On the downside, **23,600** has held twice this week — a break below would signal more weakness.
