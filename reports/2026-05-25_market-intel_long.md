# Weekly Market Report — 18 to 22 May 2026

## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty 50 closed the week at **23,746.00**, up just **+0.4%** from Monday's **23,643.75** — a flat tape that hides a noisy week underneath. The dominant catalyst was a fresh leadership change at the central bank: **Sanjay Malhotra was appointed as the new RBI Governor**, and almost immediately the RBI signalled it would **prioritise inflation management over a defensive repo rate hike**, even as the rupee hit fresh record lows on US-Iran tensions. To stabilise the currency, the RBI announced a **$5 billion dollar-rupee swap auction for May 26** — a liquidity-supportive step that markets read as dovish. Layered on top: a confidence push from the RBI bulletin and SEBI chief Madhabi Buch's "Indian markets can absorb external shocks" comment, plus a fresh **RBI proposal restricting NBFC practices** (mobile-phone disabling for defaulters, tighter board-observer scrutiny) that took a small bite out of non-bank lenders.

The week's most informative move on the **Relative Rotation Graph** (RRG — a chart that maps each sector's momentum-vs-strength into four quadrants: Leading, Weakening, Lagging, Improving) was **IT flipping from Improving to Leading** (RS Δ +1.6). With Brent crude retreating **-6.0%** to **$104.62** and the RBI flagging that the rupee will be allowed to find its level (no immediate rate hike), the export-earning, dollar-revenue IT pack got a double tailwind. **Aviation simultaneously flipped from Lagging to Improving** (+2.9%) — a clean Brent-driven rotation, since jet fuel is roughly a third of an airline's cost stack.

The flip side was uglier: **Automobile fell straight from Leading to Lagging** (RS Δ -5.1), **Cement** went Leading → Lagging (RS Δ -3.9), and **Auto Components**, **Chemicals** and **Conglomerate** all dropped out of Leading into Weakening. That's five sectors abandoning the front of the pack in a single week — a textbook rotation, not a broad-market slide. Money is leaving cyclical India-growth proxies and recycling into dollar-earners and rate-sensitives (Realty +1.9%).
