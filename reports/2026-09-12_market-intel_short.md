## The Week in One Line
Nifty slid **-2.1%** to 23,398 as a crude oil spike and West Asia tensions rattled sentiment.

## What's Working
Nothing really *worked* this week — even the "top" sectors were flat-to-red. Here are the ones that held up best:

- **Healthcare (-0.2%)**: Defensive buying held pharma steady; strong Jun quarter earnings (median EPS +26% YoY) kept the sector on investors' shopping list.
- **Defence (-0.3%)**: Rising geopolitical tension (Iran-US conflict) keeps the long-term order-book story alive; the sector's RRG position flipped to "Leading."
- **Aviation (-0.3%)**: Held up despite the crude spike — a small win, since jet fuel is aviation's biggest cost.

## What's Not
- **Textiles (-5.5%)**: Sharp reversal after months of gains; US tariff risk (the 10% temporary tariff on Indian goods) hurts export-heavy names.
- **Realty (-5.4%)**: Higher oil = higher inflation fear = higher-for-longer interest rates. Property demand is very rate-sensitive.
- **IT (-4.2%)**: Double blow — the new US tariff, plus continued weakness in TCS, Infy, HCL Tech. Infosys alone lost 8% in five days. The sector flipped from "Leading" to "Lagging."

## Notable Events

**Crude oil surged past $109/barrel** as Iran-US tensions escalated. India imports ~85% of its oil, so this hits *everything* — fuel costs feed into inflation, corporate margins, and the rupee. Auto, aviation, FMCG, and paint stocks all felt the pinch, and the Sensex fell 1,734 points across the week.

**RBI stepped in heavily** — spending over $8 billion in the spot currency market and using "currency swaps" (a tool where the RBI temporarily exchanges rupees for dollars with banks) to drain ₹9.6 lakh crore of excess cash. Goal: stop the rupee from crashing past 95/USD as oil imports get pricier. This is a big deal for banks and liquidity-sensitive sectors.

**SEBI and RBI launched "Demat 2.0"** — a pilot to put corporate bonds on blockchain-style tokens. Long-term positive for financial services and market plumbing, though not a same-week price mover.

## Charts to Know

**Breadth is deteriorating.** Breadth measures how many stocks are rising vs falling. This week, advancers stayed well below decliners every single day (Friday: 187 up, 392 down). New 52-week lows kept climbing — from 8 last week to **35 on Friday**. The share of Nifty 500 stocks trading above their 200-day average slipped from 50% to **46%**. Translation: the weakness is broad, not just a few big names.

**Nifty is now below all its key moving averages** — a "moving average" is just the average closing price over the last N days, used to spot the trend. Nifty (23,398) sits **-2.3% below its 20-day average, -2.8% below its 50-day, and -3.7% below its 200-day**. When price is below all three, the short-, medium-, and long-term trends are all pointing down. Not a panic signal, but a caution flag.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings**: No major fresh results expected — the Jun quarter reporting is done. Watch for management commentary from mid-cap AGMs.
- **Macro**: **Brent crude** is the single biggest variable — any de-escalation in West Asia could trigger a sharp relief rally. Also track the **rupee** (95/USD is the psychological line RBI is defending) and the **US Fed rate decision** flagged for the coming week.
- **Nifty levels**: Immediate support around **23,300–23,350** (this week's low zone). A break opens the door to 23,000. On the upside, Nifty needs to reclaim the 20-day EMA at **~23,950** before bulls can breathe. Everything in between is no-man's-land.

Stay patient. Down weeks are part of the deal — the goal is to survive them without doing something rash.
