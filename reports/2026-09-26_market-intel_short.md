## The Week in One Line
Nifty slipped **0.9%** to 23,140 as a **crude oil spike above $100** and RBI liquidity tightening pressured financials.

## What's Working
- **Agriculture (+2.1%)** — The Centre cut customs duty on edible oils ahead of the festive season, easing input costs and lifting agri names.
- **Realty (+1.6%)** — RBI's tweak to InvIT/REIT valuation rules and steady Jun-quarter earnings (median EPS +13%) kept buyers interested.
- **Textiles (+1.1%)** — Strong Jun-quarter results (sector median EPS **+49%**) supported the rally; textiles also flipped into the "Leading" quadrant on our relative strength map.

## What's Not
- **Conglomerate (-3.5%)** — Reliance's stagnant week and Adani Enterprises' weak post-earnings drift (-3.4% over 5 days) weighed heavily.
- **Trading (-3.2%)** — Broad risk-off sentiment as crude spiked and the rupee weakened.
- **Financial Services (-2.6%)** — A double hit: rising crude stokes inflation worry, and the RBI drained cash from the banking system (~₹72,000 crore absorbed, ~$10bn FX swaps) to defend the rupee. Less liquidity = tighter conditions for lenders and NBFCs.

## Notable Events

**Crude oil crossed $100/barrel.** Higher oil is bad news for India — we import ~85% of our crude. It pushes up inflation, hurts the rupee, and squeezes company margins. This was the single biggest reason for Thursday's sharp fall (Nifty -1.6% intraday).

**RBI actively defending the rupee.** With the US Dollar Index strengthening, the RBI conducted large FX swaps (~$10bn) and bond sales to prevent excess rupee weakness. Forex reserves fell ~$15bn to $766bn. Side-effect: banking system liquidity got tighter, which is a headwind for financial stocks.

**Edible oil duty cut — a domestic bright spot.** Ahead of the festive season, the government cut import duties on edible oils to keep food prices in check. That's directly supportive for FMCG margins and consumer wallets — and helped agri-linked stocks rally this week.

## Charts to Know

**Breadth turned choppy.** Breadth = how many stocks are rising vs falling each day. Thursday was ugly: only **86 stocks advanced vs 496 declining**, and 29 stocks hit fresh 52-week lows. Friday steadied (290 up, 292 down), but the share of stocks trading above their 200-day moving average (a long-term trend line) has slipped to **41% from 45%** early in the week. Translation: the weakness is broad, not just a couple of large stocks dragging the index.

**Nifty is now below all three major moving averages.** The 20-day EMA (23,543), 50-day EMA (23,812), and 200-day EMA (24,224) are all above the current price. Moving averages are just smoothed-out price trends — being below all of them signals the short, medium, and long-term trends have all rolled over. Not a crash signal, but a caution sign.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings calendar is quiet** — no fresh large-cap results scheduled. So price action will be **driven by macro, not earnings** this week.
- **Macro triggers:** Crude oil direction (any move back below $100 would relieve pressure), rupee vs dollar, and any update on the **US "100% tariff" Russia sanctions bill** — Trump signed it, but the tariff on India-as-Russian-oil-buyer is not yet activated. If triggered, IT, pharma and chemical exporters take the hit.
- **Nifty levels:**
  - **Support:** 23,000 (round number and psychological floor) and 22,900 (Thursday's low zone).
  - **Resistance:** 23,540 (the 20-day EMA — first hurdle for any bounce), then 23,800 (50-day EMA).
  - A close **above 23,540** would be the first sign the short-term downtrend is pausing. A break **below 23,000** opens the door to more selling.

**Quick jargon recap:** EMA = Exponential Moving Average, a smoothed price trend. RS = Relative Strength, how a sector is performing vs the market. "Leading/Lagging quadrant" = a way to visualize which sectors are gaining or losing momentum relative to the index.
