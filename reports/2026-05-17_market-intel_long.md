## 1. What Happened This Week

Indian equities had a rough five sessions. Nifty 50 closed at **23,659.35** on Friday, down **2.1%** from the previous Sunday's 24,173.95, with the bulk of the damage on Monday and Tuesday (24,173 → 23,427). The cause wasn't one single shock — it was a stack of domestic catalysts: the government **doubled the import duty on gold and silver from 6% to 15%** (effective May 13) to defend a rupee that hit a **record low**, RBI Governor flagged a likely **fuel price hike** if West Asia tensions persist, and **PM Modi appealed for citizen austerity** on fuel and gold purchases. Add Brent ticking up to **$106.98** and rolling FII outflows, and you have textbook risk-off conditions. The midweek bounce (Wednesday/Thursday) was real but couldn't reclaim Monday's losses.

## 2. The Trend Picture

The trend has clearly weakened. Nifty is now **below all three key EMAs (Exponential Moving Averages — smoothed price averages traders use as trend reference points)**:

| EMA | Level | Nifty position |
|---|---|---|
| EMA 20 | 23,889.24 | **Below** (-1.0%) |
| EMA 50 | 24,017.26 | **Below** (-1.5%) |
| EMA 200 | 24,614.61 | **Below** (-3.9%) |

This is the first time in this cycle Nifty has slipped below all three together — a meaningful change from the broad-uptrend backdrop. Breadth confirms the deterioration: **% of stocks above their 200-day EMA fell from 57% (May 7) to 43% (May 15)**. Tuesday was the panic day — **548 declines vs 36 advances** with 13 fresh 52-week lows. Wednesday and Thursday brought textbook reflex bounces (338/244 and 348/235), but Friday flipped negative again (198 adv / 385 dec). The trend is *narrowing*, not broadening — fresh 52W highs were **zero on four of the last five sessions** while 52W lows ticked up daily.

## 3. Sector Rotation — Where Money Is Flowing

Almost the entire sector board went red this week. Only two corners worked:

**TOP 3 (5-day median):**
- **ETF: +4.1%** — driven by GOLDBEES and SILVERBEES (more below)
- **TELECOM: +3.9%** — Bharti Airtel rallied 3.9% in five days despite a -38% EPS print, and Tata Communications surged into the rank-surge leaderboard
- **HEALTHCARE: -0.2%** — best of the defensive lot

**BOTTOM 3:**
- **REALTY: -8.9%** — rate-sensitive, rupee-hit
- **TRADING: -7.1%**
- **DEFENCE: -6.6%** — HAL fell -4.8% on weak earnings (more in Section 4)

The standout story is the **ETF sector flipping from Improving → Leading** with an RS Rank delta of **+11.3** (Relative Strength rank — where each sector ranks vs all others on momentum; lower number = stronger). And here's the **divergence tell**: global gold *fell* 1.5% in USD this week, yet **GOLDBEES rallied hard enough to be the #1 rank surger on Friday**. That's not a safe-haven flow story — it's the **gold/silver import duty hike** mechanically lifting the rupee landed price. Same logic for SILVERBEES (#8 rank surger). Name it directly: this is a policy-driven repricing, not investor enthusiasm.

Meanwhile, **15 sectors flipped out of Leading this week** — Banking, Financial Services, Energy, Infrastructure, Industrial, FMCG, Realty, Telecom and more all rotated to Weakening or Lagging. The one positive flip: **IT moved Lagging → Improving** (RS delta -1.4 but momentum delta +0.14) — interesting given IT was still down 6.3% on the week. Worth watching whether that's a real turn or just oversold mean reversion.

## 4. Earnings Pulse — Mar 2026 Season

This is a **Mar 2026 quarter** read. **330 stocks reported**; 212 still pending — keep that incompleteness in mind.

**(a) Sector aggregate.** Logistics is the clear winner at **+118.9% median EPS YoY** (small sample of 5). Metals (+46.5%, 16 stocks) and Consumer Durables (+27.6%, 14 stocks) are broadly strong. The weak end: **Telecom +0.9%, Defence +5.5%, Banking +9.9%** — Banking median underperformance lines up with the sector's Leading → Lagging flip.

**(b) Top-of-the-list extremes.** JSWSTEEL printed **+988% EPS YoY** (low-base distortion — the stock barely moved, +0.1% over 5 days, so the market knew). Biggest miss: SHOPERSTOP at **-922%** — colour, not signal.

**(c) Bellwether large-caps — where the real signal lives.**

- **Reliance: -13% EPS, +12.5% Rev, -6.9% over 5 days.** The headline-cap stock fell hard on a soft earnings print, confirming the conglomerate is dragging the index. With Reliance at ₹18.1 lakh crore market cap, a -6.9% week mechanically explains a chunk of the Nifty's 2.1% drop.
- **SBIN: -3% EPS, -5.5% over 5 days.** PSU bank weakness telegraphed by the country's largest. Combined with RBI cancelling 150 NBFC registrations this week, the **read-through to financial-services breadth is negative** — likely why Banking flipped Leading → Lagging.
- **Titan: +35% EPS, +80.5% Rev, but -7.5% over 5 days.** This is the most interesting tape of the week — a textbook **gold duty hike collateral hit**. Strong print, but the market priced in the demand destruction from the 6% → 15% duty hike on jewellery inputs. The move suggests forward earnings will compress; we'd want the management commentary to confirm.
- **HAL: +6% EPS, +1.8% Rev, -8.4% over 5 days.** Tiny growth on a richly-priced defence name. Sector read-through: Defence flipped Leading → Weakening, down 6.6% on the week. This is what an air-coming-out-of-the-balloon week looks like.
- **Bharti Airtel: -38% EPS but +3.9% over 5 days.** The market shrugged off a weak print because Telecom became the second-best sector. Tata Communications also surged. **The disconnect between Airtel's EPS and price is worth a flag** — possibly tariff-hike expectations or rotation flows into defensives.
- **Bajaj-Auto: +103% EPS, +41% Rev, -3.1% over 5 days.** Stellar print, but the stock sold off with the broader auto complex (M&M -6.2% on +42% EPS too). The pattern: **the market is pricing forward — austerity messaging hurts discretionary, regardless of trailing quarter strength.**

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The trend has changed posture.** Nifty below all three EMAs, breadth at 43% (from 57% a week ago), 15 sectors rotated out of Leading. This is the first genuinely weak tape of the cycle — not a crash, but a regime that no longer rewards aggressive long exposure. Caveat: 2.1% in one week is repairable if Monday opens green.

2. **Policy was the driver, not earnings.** Mar 2026 prints were broadly fine (median EPS YoY positive in 18 of 18 sectors), but Titan fell 7.5% on a +35% EPS print and Bajaj-Auto fell 3.1% on +103%. The market is pricing forward demand destruction from the gold-duty hike and the austerity message. The bellwether disconnect is the real story of the week.

3. **The rotation worth tracking is defensive.** Telecom, Healthcare and the duty-driven ETF basket led; Banking, Realty, Defence, Industrial broke down together. The MACD Buy expansion (5 → 21 unique stocks) hints at fresh momentum setups forming off the lows, but with Golden Cross Buy contracting and Blue Zone Strong slipping, we'd want one more week of broadening before calling the bottom. Honest caveat: down-week setups frequently fail in continued downtrends — confirmation, not anticipation.
