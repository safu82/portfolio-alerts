## 1. What Happened This Week

A rough five sessions for Indian equities. **Nifty 50 slid from 23,140.50 to 22,421.95, a -3.1% weekly loss**, with every single trading day closing lower than the last — Monday opened the damage at 22,780, and by Thursday's close the index had bled another 358 points without a single green bounce in between. The dominant cause isn't mysterious: **the RBI turned decisively hawkish** (BofA now forecasting 100 bps of repo rate hikes through H1 2027), **Brent spiked above $106/bbl earlier in the week on US-Iran tensions** before settling at $101.18, **Indian banks absorbed ~$500 million in FX losses** after RBI imposed position curbs on forward contracts, and the **Supreme Court declined to stay the October 15 UPI MDR (Merchant Discount Rate — the fee merchants pay on digital transactions) rollout**. Add a weak monsoon narrative and the result was textbook risk-off: breadth collapsed, rate-sensitives sold hard, and only aviation managed to stay above water.

## 2. The Trend Picture

The trend has clearly broken. Nifty at **22,421.95 is now BELOW all three key moving averages** — **-3.4% below its 20-day EMA** (Exponential Moving Average, a smoothed trend-reference line that weights recent closes more heavily) at 23,212.76, **-5.1% below the 50-day EMA** at 23,614.94, and **-7.1% below the critical 200-day EMA** at 24,125.01. When price is below all three, the medium-term trend is definitionally bearish.

Breadth confirms it. The share of stocks trading above their 200-day EMA has **compressed from 46% on Sept 23 to 33% by Oct 1** — in a single week, roughly one in seven Nifty-500 names fell below their long-term trend line. Daily advance/decline (A/D — the ratio of stocks rising vs. falling each day) has been ugly: **Monday's 72 adv / 510 dec** was the worst split of the week, and **New 52-week lows have surged from 8 (Sept 23) to 79 (Oct 1)**. Not a single new 52W high was recorded on any day this week. This is a narrowing market, not a mixed one.

## 3. Sector Rotation — Where Money Is Flowing

**Top 3 (5-day median return):** Aviation (+0.8%), Consumer Services (-1.5%), Industrial (-2.3%).
**Bottom 3:** Agriculture (-5.8%), Automobile (-5.0%), Metals (-4.9%). Cement (-4.7%) and FMCG (-4.6%) weren't far behind.

**Aviation was the only sector positive on the week** and flipped from Improving to **Leading** in RRG terms (Relative Rotation Graph — a framework that classifies sectors into Leading / Weakening / Lagging / Improving quadrants based on relative strength and momentum). The **8% weekly drop in Brent** directly helps aviation fuel costs — a clean cause-and-effect.

**Automobile's -5.0% is more interesting**. Normally cheaper crude would help autos, but the **Centre's ₹10/litre excise duty cut on petrol and diesel** (partially offset by Nayara raising retail prices by ₹5/₹3) is a mixed signal for the sector, and **Maruti fell 4.9% on the day** of its earnings read-through despite +36% revenue growth. Auto Components also flipped from Leading to **Weakening** — the rotation out of autos is happening in real time.

**FMCG's -4.6% ties to the hawkish RBI stance** — rate hikes compress consumer discretionary spend, and the group was already struggling (-19.2% YTD). **Agriculture (-5.8%) despite the edible oil import duty cut** suggests the weak monsoon narrative is overwhelming any policy tailwind.

Notable quadrant flips to watch: **Metals and Realty both flipped into Leading** despite negative weekly returns — their relative strength is improving even as absolute prices fall, which is often a precursor to a bounce. **Healthcare slipped from Leading to Weakening** — a loss of leadership despite the US specialty-drug tariff exemption news.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The trend has broken, but earnings fundamentals remain strong.** Nifty is below all three EMAs with breadth at 33% — but Jun 2026 earnings show every single sector growing double-digit EPS YoY. The disconnect is pure rates-and-macro: BofA's 100 bps forecast is doing more damage than any corporate print. Watch whether the earnings backdrop reasserts itself once the RBI narrative settles.

2. **Aviation and selective textiles are the only pockets of real strength.** Aviation flipped to Leading on the Brent drop; Welspun Living's triple-signal, 5x-volume breakout at RS #2 is the single most information-rich stock-level event of the week. Both setups need confirmation — a one-week leadership flip in a bad tape is a hypothesis, not a thesis.

3. **The signal mix says pause, not break.** MACD and Blue Zone contracting + Narrow CPR and VCP expanding = coiled consolidation, not capitulation. If the coiled cohort resolves higher next week, this week was a shakeout. If it resolves lower, the 200-day EMA at 24,125 becomes a much more distant target. Honest caveat: with VIX at just 14.46, the market isn't actually panicking yet — which can mean either the worst is over or we haven't seen the real selling. We don't know which, and anyone claiming otherwise is guessing.
