## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty 50 closed the week at **25,144.30**, up **+0.6%** from last Saturday's 24,986.40 — a modest print that masks genuine churn underneath. The dominant catalyst was a split-personality macro signal: the **RBI raised its FY27 GDP growth forecast to 7.1%** (from 6.9%) mid-week, which powered a two-day surge that took Nifty from 25,044 on Monday to **25,299 by Thursday's close** — the week's high. But Friday handed back 155 points after **October retail inflation printed at 6.1%**, breaching the RBI's 6% upper tolerance band for the first time in two years and reviving rate-hike chatter (economist consensus now sees up to 50 bps of hikes through FY27). Underneath the index-level calm, **Brent fell 3.1% to $96.14** and FIIs returned as net buyers — fuel for the sector rotation that dominated the tape.

## 2. The Trend Picture

The trend structure stays firmly constructive. Nifty closed **above its 20-day EMA (23,975, +4.9% above)**, **above its 50-day EMA (23,587, +6.6% above)**, and **above its 200-day EMA (23,968, +4.9% above)** — a textbook stacked bullish configuration (Exponential Moving Average — a smoothed average of recent closing prices that traders use to identify trend direction).

Breadth is quietly broadening. The percentage of stocks trading above their 200-day EMA climbed from **39% last Thursday to 45% by Wednesday**, settling at 41% Friday — the highest reading we've seen in weeks. **Wednesday (Nov 11) was the strongest breadth day**: 432 advances vs 148 decliners (a 2.9:1 split), coinciding with the biggest single-day index gain. Friday's A/D flipped negative (193/387) on the inflation shock, but fresh 52-week highs still outpaced lows most days. This is a market where participation is slowly expanding — narrowing on bad news, broadening on good — rather than one being carried by a few megacaps.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The trend is intact but conflicted.** Nifty is comfortably above all three EMAs and breadth is slowly broadening (41% above 200-EMA vs 37% last Thursday). But a 6.1% CPI print against a 7.1% GDP upgrade is a genuinely two-way setup — don't over-read either Wednesday's surge or Friday's fade.
2. **The rotation out of defensives into cyclicals is the real story.** Infrastructure, Financial Services, Chemicals, Realty all flipped to Improving. Healthcare and Telecom went the other way. Technical signals (37 Golden Cross Buys, 36 Blue Zone Buys, 10 fresh Bull Flags) confirm the breadth of participation. Multi-signal names like KALYANKJIL, APLLTD, LTTS and ICICIBANK are the higher-conviction expressions — worth watching for follow-through.
3. **Bellwether EPS is old; this week's price action is rotation, not reaction.** With zero fresh large-cap declarations in the last 10 days, don't credit SBI's +6% to its +20% EPS or blame TCS's -3.6% on a +15% EPS. The real driver is sector money flow, not earnings — and that flow is cyclical-friendly for now, with a rate-hike caveat looming.
