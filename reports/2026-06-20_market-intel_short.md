## The Week in One Line
Nifty climbed **+1.7%** to 24,042.85 as a US-Iran peace deal cracked oil prices and the RBI rolled out a wave of foreign-flow measures.

## What's Working
- **Trading (+9.9%)**: Stock-broker and exchange-related names surged on news the RBI is opening listed Indian equities to more foreign investors — Citi sees up to $80bn in inflows.
- **Textiles (+7.3%)**: India-US bilateral tariff deal (finalised earlier this week) makes Indian fabric exports cheaper into the US.
- **Aviation (+6.6%)**: Brent crude fell 9% to $80 — fuel is airlines' biggest cost, so cheaper oil means fatter margins.

## What's Not
- **ETF (-2.6%)**: Mostly gold ETFs — India hiked the gold import duty earlier, denting jewellery demand by an estimated 50-60 tonnes. (Note: global gold barely moved; this is a *domestic tax* story, not a safe-haven shift.)
- **Agriculture (-0.8%)**: Quiet drift lower with no fresh trigger; the sector has lagged the broader rally for weeks.
- **Metals (+0.4%)**: Flat. Aluminium downstream names are still being squeezed by India's tariff structure, which is actually pushing raw aluminium *out* of the country.

## Notable Events

**Oil crashed on a US-Iran peace deal.** Brent fell below $80/bbl, a three-month low, after the US and Iran reached an agreement that opens up the Strait of Hormuz (a key shipping route). India imports ~85% of its oil, so cheaper crude helps the rupee, eases inflation, and boosts oil-consuming sectors like aviation and paints. The Sensex jumped over 1,000 points on the news.

**RBI threw open the doors to foreign money.** Three moves in one week: (1) raised the interest rate cap banks can offer to NRIs on dollar/rupee deposits, to pull in foreign currency; (2) widened access for foreign investors to buy listed Indian equities; (3) stepped up dollar buying to rebuild reserves. Citi promptly projected **$80 billion** of inflows. This is the single biggest reason "Trading" and "Financial Services" sectors flipped to leadership this week.

**US Fed held rates, but hinted at tightening.** A tighter Fed usually hurts IT (which earns in dollars but sees US clients delay spending) and pressures the rupee. IT was the worst large sector excluding ETFs, up just **+0.9%** — Infosys and TCS dragged.

## Charts to Know

**Breadth is broadening.** The "% of stocks above their 200-day average" (a simple gauge of how many stocks are in long-term uptrends) climbed from **38% to 53%** in just seven trading days. That means the rally isn't just a few index heavyweights — more than half the market is now participating. Healthy.

**Sector rotation map.** Think of sectors as runners on a track. **Financial Services, Defence, Media, and Consumer Services** all sprinted from the back of the pack ("Lagging") into "Leading" this week. Meanwhile **Conglomerate** (think Reliance, Adani Enterprises) dropped from Leading to Lagging — Reliance's earnings showed -13% EPS and the stock has been heavy. Rotation like this is normal and usually bullish for the broader index.

## What to Watch Next Week
- **Earnings**: Mar 2026 season is wrapping up. Watch for any late-filers among mid-caps; no major bellwethers are due.
- **Macro**: (1) Whether India extends the **import duty waiver on 40 critical products** beyond June 30 — matters for petrochemicals and steel. (2) Brent crude: does it stay sub-$80, or rebound? (3) Rupee — RBI's NRI deposit move should keep it firm near 94.60.
- **Nifty levels**: The 20-day average (**23,655**) and 50-day (**23,762**) are now below price — these act as the first "support" if Nifty pulls back. Above, the **200-day average at 24,488** is the next big test — Nifty is still **1.8% below** it, and clearing it would be a strong signal for the trend.
