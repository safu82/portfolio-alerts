## The Week in One Line
Nifty rose **+1.1%** to 23,633 as a US-Iran de-escalation crashed oil prices and the RBI rolled out heavy measures to attract dollar inflows.

## What's Working
- **Aviation (+5.0%)** — Brent crude crashed 7.6% on news the US-Iran war was averted. Cheaper fuel = fatter airline margins.
- **Banking (+3.0%)** — RBI's new concessional forex swaps and higher NRI deposit rates (up to 7%) are expected to bring $20–30bn into Indian banks. Big-4 banks (HDFC, ICICI, SBI, Axis) all rallied 4–7% over five days.
- **FMCG (+1.5%)** — A rare bounce. Falling oil eases input cost worries, and softer inflation supports consumer spending.

## What's Not
- **ETF (-4.9%)** — Gold ETFs got hit as global gold fell 6.2% and silver dropped 8.1%. India's continuing 15% gold tariff is also squeezing refiners and gold-linked products.
- **Media (-3.4%)** — No specific catalyst; just persistent weakness with ad spending under pressure.
- **IT (-3.3%)** — Indian IT keeps bleeding. Concerns around US tariff negotiations and slowing US tech spend continue to weigh. The sector is now down **-23.8% YTD**.

## Notable Events

**Crude oil crashed below $90.** The biggest story of the week. A US-Iran war was averted, and Brent fell 7.6% in five days. This is a huge tailwind for India — we import ~85% of our oil. It immediately lifts aviation, paints, logistics, and the rupee, while squeezing upstream oil producers like ONGC (down 2.5% on its earnings day).

**RBI rolls out a "dollar bazooka."** The central bank launched concessional forex swaps and let banks pay NRIs up to 7% on dollar (FCNR) deposits — meant to pull $20–30bn into India. The aim: support the rupee and lower bond yields. Banks were the obvious beneficiaries. *(FCNR = Foreign Currency Non-Resident deposit — a dollar account NRIs can open with Indian banks.)*

**Duty cuts on industrial inputs.** The government scrapped customs duty on high-grade manganese ore (good for steel) and gave retrospective duty exemption on nuclear power equipment. India also signed preferential tariff access to the US market, which helps textile, auto-component, and pharma exporters.

## Charts to Know

**The breadth flip.** Look at advancers vs decliners *(simply: how many stocks went up vs down each day)*. Monday saw a brutal 70 advances vs 516 declines. Friday flipped completely: 536 advances vs 49 declines. A single huge "up day" did most of the heavy lifting for the week — narrow leadership, not broad strength.

**Nifty vs its moving averages.** Nifty closed **above** its 20-day EMA (short-term trend, +0.6%) but **below** its 50-day (-0.4%) and 200-day (-3.4%) EMAs. *(A moving average is just the average closing price over the last N days — when price is above it, the trend is up.)* Translation: short-term bounce inside a still-cautious medium-term picture.
