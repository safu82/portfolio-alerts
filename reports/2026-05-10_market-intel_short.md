## The Week in One Line
Nifty crept up **+0.7%** to **24,174**, with healthcare and defensive plays leading as easing US–Iran tensions cooled oil and lifted sentiment.

## What's Working
- **Healthcare +5.8%**: Strong Mar 2026 earnings (median EPS up ~29%) and big volume spikes in names like Metropolis and Thyrocare drew buyers into a defensive corner.
- **Aviation +5.3%**: Brent crude fell **6%** this week — cheaper jet fuel directly boosts airline margins.
- **Chemicals +4.6%**: A solid earnings season (median EPS +37%) plus rotation into beaten-down cyclicals helped the group bounce.

## What's Not
- **Banking +0.2%**: SBI fell ~6.7% post-results (only +9% EPS growth, below expectations); private banks were flat. The sector simply didn't participate.
- **Telecom +0.7%**: Mar 2026 EPS growth was nearly zero across the sector. Tejas Networks' big miss (–192% EPS) hurt sentiment.
- **Energy +1.3%**: Falling crude is bad news for oil & gas producers' revenues, even if it helps the broader economy.

## Notable Events

**US–Iran tensions eased.** Headlines flipped from "war fears" to "peace deal hopes" mid-week. Sensex jumped **940 points on Wednesday** and **1,264 points** on a separate session. Brent crude fell 6%, which is unambiguously good for India (we import most of our oil).

**Mar 2026 earnings season delivered.** Big names like **Bajaj Auto (+103% EPS), M&M (+42%), Titan (+35%), Hindustan Unilever (+21%)** beat handily. But **SBI (–3%), Reliance (–13%), and L&T (–3%)** disappointed. Cement (–8.5% median) and infrastructure (–3.2%) were the weak spots.

**State election results** added some volatility late week but didn't change the broader trend.

## Charts to Know

**The 200-day EMA wall.** EMA = "exponential moving average," basically a smoothed line of the past 200 days' prices that shows the long-term trend. Nifty (24,174) is **above** its 20-day and 50-day averages but still **1.7% below** its 200-day average (24,582). Translation: short-term momentum is healthy, but the longer-term trend hasn't fully turned bullish yet. That 24,582 level is the ceiling to watch.

**Breadth is improving but choppy.** Breadth = how many stocks rise vs. fall each day. The percentage of stocks trading above their 200-day average climbed from **48% to 58%** this week — more stocks are joining the rally, not just a few large names. That's a healthy sign. But Friday closed weak (243 advances vs 341 declines), so the trend isn't one-way.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings still trickling in**: ~317 stocks haven't reported Mar 2026 yet. Watch for misses in cement and infrastructure (already weak sectors), and confirmation in healthcare/chemicals.
- **Macro events**: Brent crude direction (any flare-up in US–Iran tensions reverses the oil tailwind), the **US Dollar Index** (currently 98.23 — a stronger dollar pressures Indian markets), and any RBI commentary.
- **Nifty levels to watch**:
  - **Support**: 24,030 (the 20-day EMA) — if Nifty stays above, the short-term uptrend holds.
  - **Resistance**: 24,582 (the 200-day EMA) — a clean break above this would be a meaningful bullish signal.
  - Below 24,000, the picture turns cautious.

**One quick education note**: a "quadrant flip" (you'll see this term in market reports) just means a sector has changed its position on a relative-strength chart. **Automobile** flipped from lagging to leading this week — a bullish rotation worth tracking. **Defence and Metals**, by contrast, flipped from leading to weakening — they had a great run but are now cooling off. Rotation is normal; no sector leads forever.
