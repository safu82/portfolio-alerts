## The Week in One Line
Nifty eked out a **+0.4%** gain to close at **22,520**, but it was a bumpy ride dominated by the **RBI's surprise rate hike** and surging crude oil.

## What's Working
- **FMCG (+2.1%)**: A defensive bounce after months of pain. Edible oil import duty cuts should ease input costs for packaged food makers.
- **Media (+1.4%)**: Quiet recovery with no major negative catalyst — buyers nibbling after a long slump.
- **IT (+1.0%)**: TCS kicked off earnings with a solid **+15% EPS** beat, and the stock jumped nearly 4%. Cooling crude also helped sentiment mid-week.

## What's Not
- **Conglomerate (-6.5%)**: Large diversified groups took the brunt of foreign selling and the rate-hike shock.
- **Realty (-3.7%)**: Rate-sensitive by nature. The RBI's hike to **5.50%** — plus guidance for *more* hikes — means costlier home loans ahead.
- **Automobile (-3.5%)**: Same story. Higher EMIs hurt car demand, and Nayara's fuel-price hike (petrol +₹5, diesel +₹3) adds to consumer stress.

## Notable Events

**RBI hiked the repo rate to 5.50%** — its first hike since 2023 — and signalled up to **100 bps more** through H1 2027. The repo rate is what banks pay to borrow from the RBI; when it rises, your home loan, car loan, and credit card rates typically rise too. This is the single biggest reason rate-sensitive sectors (banks, autos, realty) came under pressure. The RBI also said bank "liquidity surplus" (extra cash sloshing in the banking system) will shrink by year-end — another tightening signal.

**Crude oil spiked to $106/bbl** on US-Iran tensions before settling near **$103**. India imports roughly 85% of its oil, so expensive crude hits everything — fuel prices, airline costs, FMCG packaging, the rupee. Nayara Energy raised pump prices, and the government imposed a **90-day limit on bulk diesel sales** to stop hoarding.

**Edible oil duty cut** is a quiet positive. India's sunflower oil imports are projected to jump **30%** next year after the import tax was reduced. That's cheaper raw material for FMCG makers — part of why the sector led this week.

## Charts to Know

**Market breadth was ugly in the middle, strong at the edges.** Breadth simply counts how many stocks rose vs fell each day. Thursday saw just **35 stocks advance vs 548 decline** — a brutal washout as Nifty cracked below 22,250. But Friday snapped back with **406 advancers**, suggesting panic bottoming rather than a slow bleed. Only about **29% of stocks** are trading above their 200-day average — a reminder that under the surface, this market is still weak.

**Nifty vs its moving averages:** Nifty closed **1.8% below its 20-day EMA, 3.8% below the 50-day, and 6.4% below the 200-day**. A moving average is just the average closing price over the last N days — a smoothed trend line. Being below all three means the medium-term trend is still down, even after this week's modest bounce.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings season begins in earnest** — Infosys, Wipro, HDFC Bank, and Reliance are expected in the coming weeks. TCS set a positive tone; watch if the rest of IT follows.
- **Macro**: Any further RBI commentary on the rate path, crude oil moves on US-Iran headlines, and the rupee (near record lows despite RBI intervention).
- **Nifty levels**: Thursday's low near **22,230** is immediate support — if it breaks, the next cushion is well below. On the upside, **22,935** (the 20-day EMA) is the first real resistance; reclaiming it would be the first sign the correction is pausing.
