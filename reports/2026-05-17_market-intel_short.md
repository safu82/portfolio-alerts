## The Week in One Line
Nifty slipped **-2.1%** to 23,659 as a weaker rupee, rising oil, and a surprise gold/silver duty hike shook investor nerves.

## What's Working
- **ETFs (+4.1%)** — Domestic gold and silver ETFs surged after the government **doubled import duty on gold and silver** (from 6% to 15%, effective May 13). Indian-listed bullion ETFs jumped even as global gold *fell* — because new buyers must now pay a much higher onshore price.
- **Telecom (+3.9%)** — Bharti Airtel's results were taken positively by the market (stock +3.9% over five days), and the sector held up as defensive money rotated out of banks.
- **Healthcare (-0.2%)** — Barely down, which counts as winning this week. Strong Mar 2026 earnings (median EPS +19% YoY) and big beats from Neuland Labs and Wockhardt kept buyers interested.

## What's Not
- **Realty (-8.9%)** — Hit hardest. A weaker rupee plus the threat of higher fuel prices and possible H2 rate hikes is bad news for property buyers who rely on loans.
- **Defence (-6.6%)** — Profit-booking after a strong run. HAL fell sharply post-earnings (EPS only +6% — below expectations baked into the price).
- **IT (-6.3%)** — Continues its long slide (-27% YTD). Despite decent earnings from Infosys (+24% EPS) and TCS (+12%), a stronger dollar usually helps IT — but the **capital flight worry** dragged the whole market down with it.

## Notable Events

**Gold & silver import duty more than doubled.** On May 13, the government raised import duty on gold and silver from 6% to an effective 18.4%. The goal: curb gold imports (Indians have been buying heavily) and reduce demand for dollars, which is hurting the rupee. Immediate effect: domestic gold ETFs spiked higher, jewellery stocks came under pressure, and PM Modi publicly appealed to citizens to cut back on fuel and gold purchases.

**Rupee hits a record low.** The currency fell sharply on a spike in dollar demand linked to gold buying and broader capital flight (foreign investors pulling money out). A weaker rupee makes imports — especially oil — more expensive, which feeds inflation. The RBI Governor warned that **petrol and diesel prices may need to be hiked** if oil stays elevated due to the West Asia crisis.

**RBI cracks down on NBFCs.** The central bank cancelled registrations of 150 non-banking lenders. Combined with whispers that the RBI may *raise* rates in the second half of the year (instead of cutting), this hit Banking and Financial Services hard — Banking flipped from "Leading" to "Lagging" on relative strength.

## Charts to Know

**Breadth is breaking down.** Breadth measures how many stocks are rising vs falling each day. On Monday and Tuesday, decliners outnumbered advancers nearly 4-to-1 (548 down vs just 36 up on May 12). The share of stocks trading above their 200-day average — a slow-trend health check — dropped from 57% to 43% in a single week. Translation: this isn't a few large stocks pulling the index down; selling is broad.

**Nifty is now below all its key moving averages.** Moving averages are simply rolling price averages used to spot trend. Nifty closed at 23,659 — below its 20-day (23,889), 50-day (24,017), and 200-day (24,614) averages. When price slips under all three, the short-term trend is officially "down."

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings**: The Mar 2026 season is winding down (330 reported, 212 still pending). Watch laggards in Banking and IT for any late surprises.
- **Macro**: Brent crude (now $107) and the rupee are the two dials to watch. Any fresh escalation in West Asia could force a fuel price hike — bearish for almost every sector.
- **Nifty levels**: **23,400** held as support twice this week (Tuesday and Wednesday lows) — a break below opens the door to **23,000**. On the upside, Nifty needs to reclaim the 20-day average at **23,889** to suggest the worst is over.
