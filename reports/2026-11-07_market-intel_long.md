## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty 50 ripped **+8.6% in a single week**, closing Friday at **25,030.35** from Sunday's 23,053.10 — a near two-month high and one of the strongest weekly prints of the year. The dominant cause isn't mysterious: **Brent crude collapsed 11.5% to $99.18** on the back of US midterm election results, and that single commodity move cascaded into sector rotation across aviation, autos, refiners and consumer-sensitive names. Layered on top: **Reliance's blowout Q2 print** (+41% EPS YoY) gave the heavyweight index a mechanical lift, and sentiment-wise, headlines framing the midterm outcome as "the end of Trump's tariff tactics against India" eased the overhang on export-oriented sectors. Breadth confirmed the move — Monday through Thursday were all heavy advance days — but **Friday's A/D flipped negative (247/332)** even as Nifty closed higher, worth filing away.

## 3. Sector Rotation — Where Money Is Flowing

**Top 3:** Aviation (+12.3%), Conglomerate (+12.0%), Cement (+9.2%).
**Bottom 3:** ETF (+0.8%), Industrial (+1.6%), IT (+2.0%).

The rotation story reads directly off the crude chart. **Aviation flipped from Lagging straight into the Leading quadrant** (RS Δ +10.8, momentum Δ +1.91) — a two-quadrant jump on the RRG (Relative Rotation Graph — a sector-cycle map where Leading is the strongest zone). Jet fuel is the single largest variable cost for Indian carriers, and an 11.5% Brent drop flows straight to margins. **Conglomerate's +12%** is almost entirely Reliance doing the work (+13.3% over 5 days on the earnings beat and fuel-cost tailwind for its refining business).

**Auto Components, Textiles and Telecom all flipped Lagging → Improving** — consistent with the "US midterms ease tariff overhang" narrative, since these are the export-sensitive baskets. Textiles in particular posted an RS jump of +6.1 with momentum +1.55 — notable given no single catalyst in the headlines, suggesting the move is thematic rather than news-driven.

On the other side, **IT flipped Weakening → Lagging** (RS Δ **-7.5**, the week's biggest drop) despite a benign tariff narrative — a stark relative-strength loss that says money is rotating *out* of defensives into cyclicals. **Cement, counterintuitively, printed +9.2% on the sector return yet flipped Improving → Lagging on RRG** — the median stock rallied but lost relative ground to faster-moving sectors. The absolute move was good; the relative move was not.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The rally is real and the trend has reset** — Nifty above all three EMAs, MACD breadth doubled, Golden Cross count +14. But **only 38% of stocks are above their 200-EMA**, so this is still a story of the index pulling ahead of the median stock. Caveat: Friday's A/D flip (247/332) on a positive-price day is the kind of divergence that often precedes a 1-3 day pause.
2. **The rotation is crude-driven and tariff-sentiment-driven, not earnings-driven** — the earnings season was already baked in, and the "Recent Earnings Declared" list for this week is empty. If Brent reverses, the aviation/auto/refiner trade reverses with it. Worth watching second-tier confirmation: UNOMINDA, ASHOKLEY, JKTYRE rank surges are the tell for whether the auto theme has legs beyond M&M/Maruti.
3. **Watch the IT vs Pharma pair** — IT flipped Weakening → Lagging with the week's worst RS drop (-7.5), while Healthcare flipped Lagging → Improving with multi-signal confirmation in PPLPHARMA, EMCURE, GLENMARK, DIVISLAB. This looks like a classic defensive rotation inside a cyclical rally — early signs only, needs another week to confirm.
