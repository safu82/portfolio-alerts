## The Week in One Line
Nifty eked out a **+0.6% gain** to close at **24,344.75**, saved by a Friday bounce as IT rallied while rate-sensitive and cyclical sectors wobbled on inflation and tariff worries.

## What's Working
- **IT (+2.4%)**: Strong Q1 earnings previews from TCS, HCLTECH, and TECHM lifted the sector; a weaker rupee (breached 96/USD) also helps IT exporters who earn in dollars.
- **Textiles (+0.4%)**: Quiet leader — continues a multi-month run (+19.8% YTD) on steady export demand.
- **Media (+0.4%)**: A modest bounce from deeply oversold levels, not a fundamental shift.

## What's Not
- **Defence (-2.8%)**: Profit-taking after months of gains; BDL among the week's worst rank decliners.
- **Infrastructure (-2.8%)**: Hit by RBI absorbing ₹1.10 lakh crore of surplus liquidity from banks — less easy money hurts capital-heavy sectors.
- **Metals (-2.5%)**: Global demand jitters, despite JSW Steel posting a +113% EPS jump.

## Notable Events

**Inflation crossed the RBI's 4% target for the first time in 17 months.** In plain English: prices are rising faster than the central bank wants. This makes future rate cuts less likely, which is bad news for anyone hoping cheaper loans will boost consumer and real-estate demand. The RBI held rates steady at 6.5% and also pulled ₹1.10 lakh crore of spare cash out of the banking system — a tightening signal.

**A US Senate bill proposes a 100% tariff on Indian goods** as a penalty for India buying Russian oil. Nothing is law yet, but it's an overhang for exporters — textiles, pharma, and IT all sell heavily into the US. India is trying to lock in a broader US trade deal to defuse this.

**The government raised export duty on diesel (₹15.5/L) and jet fuel (₹14.5/L)** while Brent crude jumped 11% to $85 on Iran-US tensions. Double whammy for **aviation, logistics, and refiners** — higher input costs plus a tax on exporting the finished product. That's why infrastructure and transport-linked names underperformed.

## Charts to Know

**Breadth is weakening under the surface.** Breadth simply counts how many stocks went up vs down each day. Early in the week (July 9-10), advancers crushed decliners by 5-to-1. By Thursday and Friday, decliners were winning (339 vs 243, then 379 vs 202) — even as the Nifty itself rose on Friday. Translation: **the index gain was carried by a few large IT names, not broad participation.** New 52-week lows also crept up from 1 to 7. Something to watch.

**The Nifty is sandwiched between key moving averages.** A moving average is just the average closing price over the last X days — a smoothed trend line. Nifty sits **above** its 20-day (24,054) and 50-day (23,949) averages — short-term trend is up. But it's **just below** the 200-day average (24,400) — the long-term trend line. Reclaiming 24,400 would be a bullish signal; failing here keeps the market range-bound.

## What to Watch Next Week
- **Earnings**: Q1 results season is heating up. Watch large private banks, Infosys, and Bajaj Finance for the next big prints.
- **Macro**: **Brent crude** (currently $85) — any further Iran-US flare-up pushes it higher and hurts India's import bill. Also watch the **rupee** — if it slides past 96.50/USD, expect more RBI intervention headlines. And updates on the **US-India trade deal** vs the 100% tariff bill.
- **Nifty levels**: **24,400** is the ceiling (200-day EMA). A clean close above turns the trend positive. On the downside, **24,000** is the psychological floor — Tuesday's low of 24,037 already tested it. Below that, next support is around **23,950** (50-day EMA).
