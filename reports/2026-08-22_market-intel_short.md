## The Week in One Line
Nifty slipped **-0.5%** to 24,252 as a crude oil spike, hawkish RBI minutes, and fresh US tariff worries kept buyers cautious.

## What's Working
- **ETFs (+5.1%)** — Gold and silver ETFs led the pack. Global gold rose 4.6% and silver 6.9%, and Indian gold ETFs got an extra domestic tailwind from a weakening rupee.
- **Media (+2.2%)** — A quiet catch-up move after a long spell of underperformance; the sector flipped from "Lagging" to "Improving" on our relative-strength map (a tool that ranks sectors vs the broad market).
- **Textiles (+0.6%)** — Strong June-quarter earnings (median EPS growth +49% YoY) kept the sector bid, with stocks like Welspun Living showing standout momentum.

## What's Not
- **Aviation (-4.0%)** — Petrol and diesel prices were hiked ₹3/litre, and Brent crude jumped **7.6% to $93.74**. Jet fuel costs are aviation's biggest headache.
- **IT (-2.0%)** — The White House confirmed a temporary **10% tariff on India**, and US-India talks are now discussing digital-sector concessions. IT exporters are directly in the crosshairs.
- **Chemicals (-1.5%)** — A US safeguard tariff on quartz surface products, plus weakness in export-oriented specialty names, weighed on the sector.

## Notable Events

**Crude oil surge + fuel price hike.** Brent jumped to $93 on US-Iran tensions and a US Senate bill threatening sanctions on buyers of Russian crude (India is a big buyer). Domestically, petrol and diesel prices were raised ₹3/litre and Delhi CNG ₹2/litre. That's a direct hit to aviation, logistics, and anything transport-heavy — and it feeds into inflation.

**RBI holds rates, but sounds hawkish.** The RBI kept its repo rate (the rate at which banks borrow from the RBI) at **5.25%**. But the meeting minutes revealed members are worried about inflation and hinted rates could *rise* from here, not fall. Bond investors sold off. Rate-sensitive sectors (autos, realty, banks) took note — though banking still flipped to "Leading" on strong Q1 earnings.

**Sugar duty scrapped to 0%.** The government waived the 100% sugar import duty to cool record domestic prices. Bad news for sugar mills long-term — but interestingly, Shree Renuka Sugars and Balrampur Chini saw big volume spikes this week, likely on short-covering or expectations that Pakistan won't be allowed to supply.

## Charts to Know

**Breadth was weak most of the week.** Breadth simply measures how many stocks went up vs down each day. On Aug 19, only **177 stocks advanced against 406 that declined** — a lopsided down day. Only **54% of stocks are above their 200-day moving average** (a long-term trend line), down slightly from last week. Translation: the index looks calm, but under the surface more stocks are struggling than winning.

**Nifty is squeezed between its short and long trend lines.** It's just below the 20-day EMA (24,302) and well below the 200-day EMA (24,385). An EMA is a smoothed average of recent prices — think of it as the market's "cruising altitude." Right now Nifty is drifting sideways between these levels, which typically resolves with a sharp move once a catalyst arrives.

## What to Watch Next Week
- **Earnings**: The Q1 (Jun 2026) season is essentially over — no fresh declarations expected. Watch for management commentary and any pre-announcements from IT names on the US tariff impact.
- **Macro**: Any US-India trade deal update (digital concessions are on the table), Brent crude direction, and the rupee's response to RBI's FX interventions.
- **Nifty levels**: **24,178** (the 50-day EMA) is immediate support — a break below it would be a warning. On the upside, **24,385** (the 200-day EMA) is the wall bulls need to reclaim for the trend to turn positive again.
