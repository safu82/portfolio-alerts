## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty 50 eked out a **+0.6% weekly gain** (24,210.85 → **24,344.75**), but that mild green close hides a jumpy week. The index actually spent Monday through Thursday underwater — dipping to **24,036.90 on Tuesday** — before Friday's **+265-point rip** rescued the tape. The dominant themes: (a) **IT roared back to life** on the back of a weaker rupee and strong Q1 earnings previews (TCS, TechM, HCL), and (b) **domestic policy took centre stage** — the RBI held repo at 6.5% but flagged CPI breaching the 4% target for the first time in 17 months, absorbed **₹1.10 lakh crore of surplus liquidity**, and watched the rupee break past **96/USD**. Add a Washington curveball — a **US Senate bill proposing 100% tariffs on Indian goods** tied to Russian oil purchases — and a government hike in **diesel export duty to ₹15.5/L and ATF to ₹14.5/L**, and you have a week where the "why" was almost entirely policy-driven, not earnings-driven.

## 2. The Trend Picture

Nifty is sitting in a slightly awkward technical spot:

| Reference | Level | Nifty vs | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMA 20 | 24,053.78 | +1.2% | **Above** |
| EMA 50 | 23,949.07 | +1.7% | **Above** |
| EMA 200 | 24,399.85 | -0.2% | **Below** |

So the short and medium-term trend is intact, but the long-term line (EMA 200 — a smoothed 200-day average that traders treat as the bull/bear dividing line) is acting as **overhead resistance just 55 points away**. This is a classic "coiling under the 200" setup — either it breaks out and confirms a fresh leg, or it fails and rolls over.

Breadth confirms the ambivalence. Percent of stocks above their 200-day EMA is stuck in a **50-55% band all week** — neither expanding nor collapsing. Advance/Decline was **skewed negative on 4 of 5 sessions**: Tuesday was the ugliest (**144 advances vs 439 declines**, 4 fresh 52-week lows), and even Friday's rally posted **202 advances against 379 declines with 7 new 52W lows**. Translation: the index closed up, but a majority of stocks did not participate. **Narrowing breadth, index-level strength** — a warning to keep at the back of the mind.

- **TCS (EPS +5%, Rev +14%; 5-day +9.7%)** — a modest print, but the *reaction* was outsized. The market is repricing IT off a weak rupee and a low starting valuation base. TechM's **+8.1% five-day** on +28% EPS confirms the read-through: IT is being re-rated as a group, not stock-by-stock.
- **RELIANCE (EPS -22%, Rev +27%; 5-day +1.5%)** — a headline miss on EPS but revenue is roaring. Market shrugged, meaning investors are focused on the top-line and O2C recovery, not the reported earnings drop. Refining margins with Brent at $85 will be the next test.
- **FEDERALBNK (EPS +36%; 1-day +6.9%, 5-day +5.8%)** — the standout banking reaction. Combined with a **6.8x volume breakout** on Friday, this looks like fresh institutional interest. Read-through: mid-cap private banks are finding a bid even as the broader banking group faces the RBI liquidity headwind.
- **BHEL (EPS +182%; 1-day -3.1%, 5-day +6.8%)** — the initial reaction was disbelief; the five-day recovery says the market eventually accepted the number. Consistent with the **Industrial cluster's +32% EPS median**.
- **JIOFIN (EPS +147%, Rev +227%; 5-day +0.4%)** — a monster print met with almost no reaction. That non-reaction, plus **7.3x volume** on Friday, hints the market is not sure what to do with it (still finding a valuation frame for the vertical mix).

**Staleness check:** Only **JSWSTEEL (Jul 17) and POLYCAB (Jul 16)** actually declared in the last 10 days. So for all other bellwethers above, the earnings prints are older; **this week's price action is sector-rotation flow, not a fresh earnings reaction**. Frame accordingly.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The index rose but breadth didn't.** Nifty closed +0.6%, yet 4 of 5 sessions had negative advance/decline. Rotation is happening under the surface — IT and consumer durables are being bought, financials and defence are being sold. Watch whether the EMA 200 at 24,400 acts as a lid or a launchpad next week.

2. **IT is the freshest positive story, and it's rupee-driven, not earnings-driven.** With INR at 96 and DXY flat, dollar-revenue exporters get a mechanical tailwind. TCS's +9.7% five-day on a modest +5% EPS print is the tell. Worth watching whether the move broadens to mid-cap IT (ECLERX, LTTS) or exhausts.

3. **The RBI liquidity drain + CPI breach are the sleeper bearish catalysts.** Everyone is watching Brent and the US tariff headline, but ₹1.10 lakh crore pulled out of the system and inflation crossing 4% for the first time in 17 months is the quiet structural pressure on financials, realty, and rate-sensitives. Caveat: one week doesn't make a trend, and the earnings season is only 39 of 545 stocks in — verdict pending.
