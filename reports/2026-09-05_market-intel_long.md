## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty 50 slid **-1.1%** for the week, from **24,175.65** on Sunday to **23,897.70** by Friday's close — a fourth straight weekly loss. The dominant catalyst was the **Iran-US conflict flare-up, which spiked Brent crude +9.0% to $95.37**, torching import-sensitive sectors (Auto, Aviation, FMCG). Cushioning the fall: **India's Q1 FY27 GDP printed at 7.8%**, comfortably beating RBI's estimate, and the **RBI's forex swap window drew a bumper $136 billion** (with FCNR(B) deposits crossing $100 billion vs the $80bn expectation) — a genuinely large capital-flows story that stabilised the rupee. Add in **SEBI clearing NSE's ₹30,000 crore IPO** (India's second-largest ever), and the week was a tug-of-war between a nasty oil shock and unusually strong domestic macro plumbing.

## 3. Sector Rotation — Where Money Is Flowing

**Top 3 (5-day median):** Trading (+1.9%), Textiles (+0.9%), Logistics (+0.2%).
**Bottom 3:** Automobile (-4.8%), Conglomerate (-4.4%), Aviation (-3.2%), with FMCG (-2.6%) close behind.

The **crude spike does most of the work here**. Aviation and Auto — the two most obvious fuel-sensitive sectors — sit at the bottom. FMCG's slide reflects the same input-cost pressure plus a broader risk-off tilt. Conglomerate's -4.4% is heavily influenced by Adani names (see Section 4).

Quadrant flips (RRG — Relative Rotation Graph — is a dashboard that tracks sectors across four quadrants: Leading, Weakening, Lagging, Improving) are unusually numerous this week — **17 sectors moved quadrants**, telling you the rotation is happening in real time:

- **Into Leading:** IT (from Weakening), Healthcare (from Weakening), Conglomerate (from Improving) — a defensive rotation.
- **Out of Leading:** Banking → Weakening, Defence → Weakening, ETF → Weakening, Realty → Weakening, Industrial → Weakening.
- **Automobile: Weakening → Lagging** — the worst kind of flip, confirming the crude damage.
- **Energy: Lagging → Improving** — the natural crude beneficiary.

IT and Healthcare both re-entering Leading is textbook defensive rotation into a weak-tape week. But note: IT also carries **tariff overhang** from Piyush Goyal's ongoing US trade-deal negotiations — the sector's leadership status is fragile.

## 8. What to Watch Next Week

- **Nifty levels:** the 20/50 EMA cluster near **24,155–24,166** is now overhead resistance. Friday's close at **23,897.70** needs to hold; a decisive break below would open air toward the recent lows. On the upside, reclaiming 24,155 would be the first sign of trend repair.

- **Continuation themes:** whether IT and Healthcare hold their fresh Leading-quadrant status, and whether Banking's slip from Leading → Weakening deepens or reverses. The Narrow CPR breakaway coil (52 stocks) is a setup queue — watch for which of them actually resolves higher.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The tape is decisively below trend — but the macro underneath is stronger than the price action suggests.** Nifty is below its 20/50/200 EMAs and breadth is thin, yet Q1 GDP printed 7.8% and RBI pulled in $136bn of FX. The gap between price and fundamentals is unusual; resolution will hinge on Brent.
2. **Crude sensitivity is the dominant sector filter right now.** Auto flipped Weakening → Lagging, Aviation and FMCG both sold off, while Energy improved. Until Brent settles, factor exposure to fuel costs is the single biggest driver of relative performance — even ahead of earnings.
3. **The signal mix is rotating, not expanding — favour the multi-signal names.** Blue Zone contracted by 12 stocks even as Narrow CPR breakaways expanded by 24. In a narrowing market, cross-indicator confirmation matters more; **DIVISLAB, UFLEX, COFORGE, KOTAKBANK, BAJAJ-AUTO** carry the strongest technical stack. Honest caveat: in a weak-tape week, even good setups fail more often — position sizing matters as much as selection.
