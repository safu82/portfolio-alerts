## The Week in One Line
Nifty slipped **-1.1%** to 23,897.70, dragged by an oil-price shock and a tariff scare, even as India's GDP surprised on the upside.

## What's Working
- **Trading (+1.9%)**: Lone bright spot as brokers and exchange-linked names caught a bid on news that SEBI cleared NSE's **₹30,000 cr IPO** — India's second-largest ever.
- **Textiles (+0.9%)**: Kept its strong 6-month run (+13%) going. Sector earnings this quarter were up **~49% YoY**, keeping investors interested.
- **Logistics (+0.2%)**: Barely positive, but notable — logistics companies posted the strongest earnings growth of any sector this quarter (**+81% median EPS**).

## What's Not
- **Automobile (-4.8%)**: Worst sector of the week. Fear that a bumpy **India-US trade deal** could bring fresh tariffs hurt auto exporters; Maruti and M&M both fell ~5%.
- **Conglomerate (-4.4%)**: Adani Enterprises slid over **7%** across the week on continued weakness after weak reported earnings.
- **Aviation (-3.2%)**: A **9% jump in Brent crude** to $95 (on Iran-US tensions) directly threatens airline fuel costs.

## Notable Events

**Oil spike from Iran-US flare-up.** Renewed conflict between Iran and the US pushed Brent crude up **9% in a week**. India imports most of its oil, so a spike like this hurts everyone — higher input costs for manufacturers, pricier fuel for airlines, and pressure on the rupee. This was the single biggest reason for the four-day losing streak mid-week.

**India-US trade deal wobble.** Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal is scrambling to negotiate preferential tariffs before a final US trade deal is signed. Uncertainty here is bad news for anyone selling to America — IT services, auto exporters, pharma, and engineering firms. This is why Autos got hammered.

**GDP surprise + RBI's cash flood.** On the positive side, India's Q1 GDP came in at **7.8%**, beating the RBI's own forecast. Separately, the RBI's forex swap facility pulled in a massive **$136 billion** in foreign deposits — much more than the $80bn expected. That's a huge vote of confidence in the rupee and India's financial stability. The market simply didn't reward it this week because oil and tariff fears dominated.

## Charts to Know

**Breadth is holding, barely.** The advance/decline line — which counts how many stocks went up vs down each day — was mostly negative through the week (~200 advancers vs ~380 decliners on down days), but Wednesday and Thursday flipped positive. The percentage of stocks trading above their long-term 200-day average has held steady near **50%**. Translation: the market is weak but not breaking — roughly half of listed stocks are still in medium-term uptrends.

**Sector rotation is chaotic.** A tool called RRG (Relative Rotation Graphs) tracks which sectors are gaining or losing strength vs the broader market. This week saw **17 sectors change quadrants** — a very high churn. Money is fleeing Autos, Defence, and ETFs and rotating into Healthcare, IT, and Energy. When rotation is this scattered, it usually means investors don't have a clear conviction — a signal to stay patient.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings**: The Jun-2026 reporting season is essentially over. No major bellwether prints expected. Focus shifts to management commentary and any pre-quarter guidance updates.
- **Macro**: Brent crude direction (any Iran-US de-escalation would be a big relief); progress on the India-US trade deal; RBI's next liquidity operation as it manages the $136bn inflow.
- **Nifty levels**: Nifty closed below all three key moving averages (20-day, 50-day, and 200-day EMAs — the trend indicators most traders watch). Support is around **23,850**; a break below opens 23,500. On the upside, Nifty needs to reclaim **24,155** (the 50-day EMA) to signal the down-move is done.
