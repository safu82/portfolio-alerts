## The Week in One Line
Nifty slipped **-0.6%** to 23,605 as a sharp Friday sell-off (448 decliners vs 136 advancers) wiped out a strong start to the week.

## What's Working
- **Auto Components (+3.5%)**: Solid Mar-2026 earnings (median EPS +37% YoY) kept buyers interested — names like Exide and Asahi India saw fresh demand.
- **Telecom (+3.0%)**: The sector flipped into "Leading" on our relative-strength map (a simple ranking of which sectors are beating the broad market). Strong earnings season — median EPS up 43% YoY — is doing the heavy lifting.
- **Industrial (+1.9%)**: Capex names continue to grind higher; the sector is up 12% over three months on the back of government infrastructure spending.

## What's Not
- **Agriculture (-5.8%)**: Sharp reversal after a strong 6-month run; profit-taking ahead of monsoon uncertainty flagged by the Finance Ministry.
- **Logistics (-4.1%)**: Container Corp (CONCOR) led the decline. The sector flipped from "Weakening" to "Lagging" — meaning it's now both underperforming AND losing momentum.
- **Conglomerate (-2.1%)**: Reliance continues to weigh after its weak earnings print (-13% EPS YoY) earlier in the reporting season.

## Notable Events

**RBI's record dividend transfer (₹2.87 lakh crore) to the government.** In plain English: the central bank made big profits (mainly from currency operations) and handed them to the Finance Ministry. This gives the government more room to spend without borrowing heavily — usually positive for growth-linked stocks. Combined with the RBI's 6.9% FY27 growth forecast, it sets a "cautious resilience" backdrop for equities.

**Cotton import duty waived.** The government scrapped duties on cotton imports to support the textile sector. Cheaper raw material = better margins for apparel and textile companies. A reminder that domestic policy decisions can move sectors as much as earnings do.

**Rupee weakness + dollar funding stress.** Indian banks are asking the RBI for help with the cost of hedging dollar loans. The RBI Governor himself hinted the rupee may be "undervalued." This matters because a weak rupee pressures import-heavy sectors (oil, electronics) while helping exporters (IT, pharma). It's also why banking flipped from "Lagging" to "Leading" this week — investors are pricing in stability under the new RBI Governor.

## Charts to Know

**The breadth chart tells the real story.** Breadth simply counts how many stocks went up vs down each day. Monday looked fantastic — 440 stocks up, only 145 down. Then Friday flipped violently: only 136 stocks up, 448 down. When breadth swings this hard in a single session, it often means institutions are repositioning quickly, not a slow trend change. Watch next week to see which way it settles.

**Nifty vs its moving averages.** A moving average is just the average closing price over the last N days — it shows the underlying trend. Nifty is now below all three key averages: the 20-day (short-term), 50-day (medium), and 200-day (long-term). Being below the 200-day by 4% is the cautionary signal — the longer-term trend has weakened.
