## 1. What Happened This Week

Nifty ended the week at **24,270.55**, up **+0.9%** from Sunday's 24,056 close, with the real work done on **Thursday (+0.8%) and Friday (+0.4%)** after a soft start Monday-Tuesday. Two catalysts drove the rebound, both fresh this week: **Brent crude fell -2.7% to $72.20** on Iran-US diplomatic easing and Strait of Hormuz de-escalation (lifting oil importers and IT), and the **RBI held the repo rate unchanged** while trimming its FY26 real GDP projection to **6.9%** (from ~7%) — a mildly dovish hold that stabilised banking sentiment even as the RBI's Financial Stability Report flagged AI-linked global valuation risk and energy-import exposure. A third domestic policy nudge — the government's **extension of customs duty exemption on critical petrochemical imports** to mid-July — quietly supported the chemicals complex.

## 9. The Bottom Line

1. **The rally is broadening at the accumulation end while narrowing at the entry end.** Blue Zone Buy expanded to 77 stocks and Blue Zone Strong tripled to 17, but MACD Buy collapsed from 40 to 17. Second-innings behaviour — supportive but not the setup for chasing.

2. **Healthcare's Weakening → Leading flip is the cleanest positive rotation on the board this week** (RS Δ +3.2, momentum Δ +0.81), matched by the sector's +2.6% 5d return and cluster signal activity in KIMS and LAURUSLABS. Worth watching for follow-through — pharma is also in the India-US trade deal crosshairs, so the July 24 deadline could either extend or interrupt the move.

3. **Banking's Leading → Weakening flip despite the RBI hold is the most honest contradiction in the data.** With the sector median EPS at just +9.9% (the weakest of the reporting universe outside Defence) and AXISBANK/KOTAKBANK dragging -2.5% and -3.0% in five days, the message is that a dovish hold isn't enough to overcome earnings gravity. Caveat: this could reverse quickly if FCNR(B) inflows and the sub-2% NPA baseline get repriced positively.

Honest limitation: with no fresh earnings in the last 10 days, all bellwether price action this week is rotation and macro-driven, not earnings-driven. That's a cleaner tape to read — but also one that can shift quickly when the July earnings window opens.
