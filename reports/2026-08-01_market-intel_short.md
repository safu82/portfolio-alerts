## The Week in One Line
Nifty gained **+2.4%** to close at 24,363, powered by a relief rally as India dodged the worst of US tariff threats and strong industrial data lifted growth hopes.

## What's Working
- **Automobile (+5.9%)**: Strong Q1 earnings from M&M (+34% EPS) and Bajaj Auto (+46%) plus a weaker rupee helping exporters.
- **IT (+5.1%)**: India got placed in the lower 10% US tariff tier with $87 billion of exports exempted — a big relief for IT services that depend on US clients.
- **Media (+4.4%)**: A quiet bounce-back sector; benefited from improving risk appetite as broader sentiment turned positive.

## What's Not
- **Agriculture (-4.7%)**: Rotation out of defensive sectors as investors chased growth stories.
- **Cement (-1.4%)**: Weak momentum despite decent earnings (UltraTech +17% EPS); the sector flipped from "Leading" to "Lagging" on relative strength.
- **ETF (-1.3%)**: Domestic gold ETFs cooled off even as global gold rose 2.6% — likely a domestic tax/duty adjustment weighing on Indian gold instruments specifically.

## Notable Events

**India catches a tariff break.** The US placed India in its lower 10% tariff tier, exempting **$87 billion of Indian exports** from additional duties. This was the week's biggest positive — it removed a major overhang for exporters, especially pharma, IT and textiles. But there's a catch: the US Senate advanced a separate Russia Sanctions Bill that could impose 100% tariffs on countries buying Russian oil (India being a big one). So relief now, uncertainty still ahead.

**Fuel prices hiked.** The government raised **petrol and diesel by ₹3/litre** and CNG in Delhi by ₹2. This is a headwind for aviation, logistics, auto and anything transport-heavy — and adds to consumer inflation. Aviation still managed +3.9% for the week on other tailwinds, but this is a slow drag to watch.

**Growth data surprises up.** Strong June industrial production data raised expectations of **Q1 FY27 GDP growth above 7%**. That's the backdrop for the week's rally — a "growth is back" narrative combined with the tariff relief.

## Charts to Know

**Nifty vs its moving averages** — a moving average is just the average closing price over the last N days, used to smooth out noise and spot trends. Nifty closed **above** its 20-day and 50-day averages (short/medium-term uptrend intact) but is still slightly **below** the 200-day average (24,426) — the long-term trend line. Reclaiming the 200-day is next week's big psychological test.

**Breadth improved but stayed choppy.** Breadth just measures how many stocks went up vs down each day. This week alternated between strong up-days (450+ advances) and weak days (400+ declines) — meaning the rally was real but uneven. The share of stocks trading above their 200-day average nudged up from 50% to 55% — healthier participation, but still not broad-based conviction.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings**: Q1 results season is winding down but mid-cap results still trickle in. Watch consumer services (Sapphire, Devyani printed +800%/+300% EPS) and healthcare (Glenmark +931%) for continued momentum.
- **Macro**: Any progress on the **India-US trade deal**, movement on the Russia Sanctions Bill in the US Senate, and Brent crude (dropped 12.5% this week to $87.80 — a huge tailwind if it holds).
- **Nifty levels**: Support around **24,080** (the 20-day average). Resistance at **24,426** (the 200-day average) — a clean break above this would confirm the medium-term trend has turned.
