## The Week in One Line
Nifty gained **+2.0%** to close at **23,053**, with aviation and healthcare leading a broad recovery after a shaky start.

## What's Working
- **Aviation (+7.2%)**: A sharp bounce as crude oil cooled off from its highs — jet fuel is a huge cost for airlines, so cheaper oil = fatter margins.
- **Healthcare (+5.0%)**: Defensive buying as investors sought steadier earnings; several pharma names (Sanofi, Ajanta, Apollo Hospitals) saw big volume spikes.
- **Media (+3.2%)**: Rebound off deeply oversold levels — the sector is still down 12% over a year, so even small buying moves it fast.

## What's Not
- **Agriculture (−9.2%)**: The sector slid hard after cotton import duty was extended — cheaper foreign cotton pressures domestic farmers and agri-input stocks.
- **ETF (−3.1%)**: Gold and silver ETFs weighed on the group as global precious metals fell (silver down 6.4%).
- **Consumer Durables (−1.5%)**: Weak demand signals and food inflation worries (less wallet room for big-ticket purchases) kept pressure on.

## Notable Events

**US Fed holds rates — but two members want a hike.** The US Federal Reserve kept interest rates steady, but the hawkish tone (two members open to raising rates) spooked global markets early in the week. Higher US rates typically pull foreign money out of Indian stocks. Nifty fell nearly 1% mid-week before recovering as the dust settled.

**Government reshuffles fuel duties.** India scrapped the export duty on petrol but *hiked* levies on diesel and aviation turbine fuel (ATF). Good news for refiners on the petrol side; a cost headwind for transport and airlines on the diesel/ATF side. Despite the ATF hike, aviation stocks still rallied — crude's drop more than offset it.

**Cotton import duty exemption extended to December 31.** Textile exporters get cheaper raw cotton (margin boost — textiles finished +2.0% on the week). But domestic cotton farmers face more competition, which hammered the agriculture sector.

## Charts to Know

**Market breadth tells the real story.** Breadth measures how many stocks are rising vs falling each day. The week started ugly: on Oct 23, only **43 stocks advanced vs 539 declining**. By mid-week, that flipped — Oct 27 saw **404 advances vs 177 declines**. Friday pulled back again (111 up, 469 down), so the rally isn't fully broad-based yet. Also telling: the number of stocks hitting fresh **52-week lows dropped from 143 on Monday to just 16 by Thursday** — a sign that selling pressure was exhausting itself.

**Nifty's position vs its moving averages.** The index is now *above* its 20-day EMA (short-term trend positive) but still *below* its 50-day and 200-day EMAs (medium and long-term trends still cautious). Translation: a decent bounce, but not yet a confirmed trend reversal.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Earnings winding down**: Most big names (Reliance, HDFC Bank, TCS, Infy, L&T) have already reported strong Sep-quarter numbers — telecom (+86% EPS), energy (+53%), and auto (+42%) led. Watch mid-cap names still to report.
- **Macro events**: Crude oil direction (currently $112, down from $116) remains the single biggest swing factor — any Iran-related escalation would spike it. Also watch rupee movement (now 94.93/USD) and any RBI commentary on rate policy.
- **Nifty levels**: The 50-day EMA at **~23,123** is the immediate resistance to clear — a close above it would signal medium-term momentum turning up. On the downside, the 20-day EMA at **~22,811** is key support. Below that, 22,600 (last Sunday's close) is the line in the sand.

**Jargon buster:** *EMA (Exponential Moving Average)* is a running average of recent closing prices that gives more weight to the latest days — traders use it as a trend reference line. *RS (Relative Strength)* measures how a stock performs vs the broader market. *Breadth* = how many stocks are participating in a move.
